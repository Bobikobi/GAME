### 2. What defines the style

1. Proportions: adult, 7-7.5 heads (MU, L2 humans), stretched to 8 for L2 elves and Aion Elyos; small head, long legs, narrow waist; never chibi or big-head.
2. Faces: semi-realistic, East-Asian idealised - smooth skin, straight nose, large-but-not-anime eyes, no age or asymmetry; on 2003 models mostly a painted texture patch on a low-poly head.
3. Armour: fantasy plate in layered sets that grow with tier - oversized pauldrons, horns, wings, spikes, long capes; asymmetric accents; medieval-fantasy, never historical.
4. Silhouette: V-torso with pauldron-widened shoulders (male), hourglass with exposed midriff/thigh (female); weapons 1.2-1.5x body length; class readable at distance by cape/wing/weapon outline, not colour.
5. Polycount: 2003 tier 1,000-3,000 tris (sourced: an extracted L2 base body is 3,049 tris; Blizzard's 2004 WoW gnome 956 / dwarf 1,160 polygons, 130 bones); ~2010 tier 5,000-10,000 tris with normal maps (Blizzard 2014 remakes: 5,408 / 7,821 polygons, 196 bones). No published MU Online figure - estimate.
6. Textures: diffuse only, no normal/PBR; shading and specular painted by hand; 128-512 px per part, power-of-two.
7. Rigging and gear: few bones (~130); MU attaches armour as separate rigid meshes per slot (helm, armor, pants, gloves, boots on one skeleton) - the cheapest route to visible gear in Three.js too.
8. Palette and presentation: desaturated steel/leather/earth base, one or two saturated accents (gold trim, blue/red/purple glow on high tier); 3/4 heroic stance, weapon drawn, camera slightly below eye level, rim light, dark gradient background.

### 4. The rules

1. Style vs character: the MU look (7-8-head adult body, semi-real face, plate-and-pauldron vocabulary, glowing weapons, palette, pose) is an idea and is free; the specific drawing, mesh, texture and ornament layout of a MU/L2 asset is a protected artistic work - Israel Copyright Act 5768-2007 s.5 (no copyright in "an idea; procedures and methods"), s.4(a)(1), CA 7996/11 Safecom v. Raviv (https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/safecom-ltd-v-raviv); US 17 U.S.C. 102(b), Atari v. North American Philips, 672 F.2d 607 (https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/672/672.F2d.607.81-2920.html).
2. The infringement test is access + substantial similarity, qualitative not quantitative, "the judge's impression of the works as a whole"; a model "substantially based upon" a MU set is a derivative work - s.11(1), s.11(6), s.16, s.47; statutory damages up to NIS 100,000 per infringement without proof of damage, s.56(a) (Safecom, same URL). Keep a dated "difference log" of deliberate departures from every reference you looked at - that is the evidence that defeats the inference.
3. Credit does not cure: the statutory test is the owner's CONSENT (s.47); attribution is a separate moral right (s.46(1)) you can satisfy while still infringing the economic right; US 17 U.S.C. 501(a) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/501) - none of the s.106 rights is conditioned on credit.
4. Looking: a reference image kept on the artist's disk for private study is a reproduction (s.12(1) "storage ... through electronic means") excused as fair use for "private study, research" (s.19(a), factors s.19(b); https://en.sce.ac.il/filestock/file/1515307007300-0.pdf). Committing it to the public repo is "making available to the public" (s.11(5)) and is not excused. Webzen ToS s.4 also forbids downloading its content even privately - keep the mood board as a list of URLs plus your own sketches, not saved files.
5. Tracing, overpainting or using a render/screenshot as a texture: a derivative work and a literal reproduction reserved to the owner (s.16, s.11(6), s.12(3) "two dimensional copy of a three dimensional work", s.47; US 17 U.S.C. 101/106(2), Steinberg v. Columbia Pictures - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101). Not shippable, no fair-use purpose fits a commercial game asset.
6. Rips: an extracted MU/L2/WoW model is an "infringing copy" (s.1); using it is direct infringement (s.47) and distributing it with knowledge is s.48 infringement; a CC or "free" label on it is void (CC BY-SA 4.0 s.1(h) "limited to ... rights ... that the Licensor has authority to license"; Sketchfab Terms s.4.4, s.5(9), s.8 - https://sketchfab.com/terms); extraction from a protected client may breach US 17 U.S.C. 1201(a)(1)(A). Treat any downloadable recognisable commercial-game asset as a rip unless the page belongs to the studio.
7. Trademarks: "MU" is a live US registration of Webzen Inc. for computer game software and online game services (USPTO Reg. 3,360,044 - https://gleanmark.com/trademark/uspto-79033423); never in the game's name, class names, URLs, store listing or tags (Israel Trade Marks Ordinance s.1 "infringement", s.46; US 15 U.S.C. 1114(1)(a)). "Dark Knight" is DC Comics' mark for comics only (Class 16; its game-class applications were abandoned 2021 - https://gleanmark.com/trademark/uspto-87730390) - low risk as a class label, but reproducing MU's roster (Dark Knight / Dark Wizard / Fairy Elf / Magic Gladiator / Dark Lord) together with the MU look is passing-off evidence under the Commercial Torts Law 5759-1999 s.1(a) (https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/128055); Korea's 2023 NCSoft v. Webzen (R2M) ruling awarded KRW 1bn under unfair competition even where nothing was copyrightable. Use your own roster.
8. CC licences: CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA (3.0 or 4.0) may be committed to the public repo with TASL attribution per CC BY(-SA) 4.0 s.3(a)(1) (Title - Author - Source - License, note modifications; https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Recommended_practices_for_attribution); BY-SA 3.0 assets you modify may be released as BY-SA 4.0 (3.0 s.4(b) "a later version"), unmodified ones stay 3.0; NC is out because the project "may have a business model" and NC-SA can never become BY-SA (CC BY-NC 4.0 s.1, s.2(a)(1)); ND is out because rigging/retopo/retexture is Adapted Material you may "produce ... but not Share" (CC BY-ND 4.0 s.2(a)(1)(B) - https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nd/4.0/legalcode); CC0 waives copyright but not trademarks and clears no third-party rights (CC0 s.4(a),(c)).
9. CC BY-SA 4.0 + AGPL-3.0: ShareAlike binds only Adapted Material, not a collection (BY-SA 4.0 s.1(a), s.3(b)(1)); AGPL is NOT on https://creativecommons.org/compatiblelicenses (GPLv3 is, one-way from BY-SA), so keep code and art as two separate works in the bundle and never adapt one into the other; CC itself "recommend[s] against using Creative Commons licenses for software". Keep a provenance ledger per asset (source URL, author, licence + version, download date, screenshot of the licence line) - CC rights "terminate automatically" on breach (BY-SA 4.0 s.6(a)) and Sketchfab binds you to "the license that applies at the time of download" (Terms s.4.2.2).
10. Store licences vs the public repo: Unity Asset Store (EULA 2.2.1 "incorporated and embedded", 2.2.1.1 no extraction - https://support.unity.com/hc/en-us/articles/34387186019988-Can-I-use-assets-from-the-Asset-Store-with-other-engines), Sketchfab Standard (no "stand-alone file" - https://sketchfab.com/licenses), Fab (s.4(c) "must restrict end users from extracting" - https://www.fab.com/eula), Adobe Mixamo (General Terms 3.6 not "on a stand-alone basis" - https://www.adobe.com/legal/terms.html) and Daz (Interactive License s.3.0 non-native formats only - https://www.daz3d.com/eula) all allow a SHIPPED game and all forbid the raw file in a public repo; such an asset is a proprietary exception that must be named in the content licence notice, never placed in the CC BY-SA folder.
11. AI-generated characters: in the US an unedited generated mesh/texture has no author and no copyright - US Copyright Office guidance 88 FR 16190 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-03-16/html/2023-05321.htm), Part 2 report 29.1.2025 (prompts "do not provide sufficient human control"), Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Cir. 2025) - so a raw generation labelled "CC BY-SA 4.0" is in truth public domain and unenforceable; only the human layers (hand retopo, repainted textures, re-sculpted anatomy, your own concept fed in) are yours. Israel's only official text, the MoJ opinion of 18.12.2022 (https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/machine-learning/he/18-12-2022.pdf), covers TRAINING (likely fair use) and expressly not OUTPUT - an output substantially similar to a MU set is judged under s.11/s.47 like any copy, and the prompt log is evidence of access (Disney v. Midjourney, filed 11.6.2025, rests on named characters emerging from prompts - https://www.thefashionlaw.com/disney-and-universal-sue-midjourney-over-ai-copyright-free-riding-scheme/). Prompt from your own design brief (armour terms, head ratios, polycount, palette), never from "MU", "Lineage" or "Webzen"; keep prompt, seed and output with the asset; route every generation through a documented human pass before it enters the repo.

### 5. Recommendation

1. Build the characters yourself in the style, not from the assets: body from a CC0 base (MakeHuman sliders set to 7.5-8 heads, or Blender Human Base Meshes / pistachio / Drummyfish), own modular plate sets per slot (helm, armor, pants, gloves, boots as separate rigid meshes on one skeleton - the MU method), hand-painted diffuse 256-512 px, 2-5k tris; mood board = the 8 URLs in section 1 plus your own sketches and a dated difference log, kept outside the repo. Licence cost: $0 (Blender, MakeHuman, all CC0); the cost is artist time or a commission.
2. If commissioning: brief a freelancer with the section 2 trait list and the section 1 URLs as "look, do not copy", and demand CC BY-SA 4.0 (or assignment) in writing. Market range (third-party figures, not from the material): simple game character $500-$1,000, detailed $1,500-$5,000, rigging +$500-$1,000 per character, freelancers $20-$100/h (https://juegostudio.com/blog/character-design-cost, https://www.hitem3d.ai/ai-faq/what-is-the-cost-of-a-weight-painting-3d-model-for-character-artists); a base body plus one modular armour set and a shared rig is the unit to buy once, then clone sets.
3. Placeholder now, at $0: System G6 "Modular RPG Characters" (CC0, 2-3k tris, ~45 animations, weapon sockets, male + female) for gameplay and rig/animation plumbing, with Danimal's adventurer/militia kit (CC BY-SA 3.0) as a second body; both may sit in the public repo today with an ATTRIBUTION line.
