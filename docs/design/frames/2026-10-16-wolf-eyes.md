# The bank wolf's eyes at night: three stills for the owner

At night the bank wolf of NEXT 12 shows only its eyes, and the player learns its round by them (G47). Today each eye is one game pixel of the orange-red the fire uses, pulsing between 55% and 95% strength about 1.4 times a second, and the eye pairs that open at the forest edge at night are the same pixels in the same red. A play audit of the south bank found the wolf's eyes hard to pick out for that reason. These stills try the wolf's eyes in a red of their own, one difference each. Nothing here is in the game yet.

- `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-1-base.png`, `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-2-size.png`, `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-3-glow.png`: the three stills.
- `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-crops.png`: the eyes side by side, 48x32 CSS px around them enlarged six times: today's eyes in the same frame on the left, caught at the faintest point of their pulse, then 1, 2 and 3, and an ambient pair from the same frame on the right.

## What each still tests

Sizes are in game pixels. On a phone the game draws one game pixel as 2x2 CSS px, so today's eyes are already 2x2 CSS px, and "2x2" here means 4x4 CSS px.

1. **base**: the eyes in the new red, 2x2 each, steady at full strength (today's pulse between 55% and 95%), no glow. Tests the red and the 2x2 eye.
2. **size**: as 1, with 3x3 eyes. Tests a bigger eye.
3. **glow**: as 1, plus a soft glow drawn the way the lantern's is: whole-pixel rings 1, 2 and 3 pixels out, the red added as light at 40%, 20% and 8%. Tests a glow.

Everything else is as it is today, the ambient pairs included. The eyes sit where today's do, ahead on the wolf's heading, turned on the east-west legs.

## The moment

One frame on a 390x844 phone at 21:59, the same in all three: the player on the tall grass at the south end of the bank with the lantern lit, the wolf on its round at (11, 27) coming south, six tiles north of the player and outside the lantern's light, and an ambient pair at the right edge. The game was paused on that frame and only the eyes were redrawn between the captures, so the stills match pixel for pixel outside the eyes. A second capture of still 1, taken last, matched the first exactly. Today's eyes were drawn in the same paused frame, at the faintest point of their pulse.

## The numbers

Measured on the stills unless marked as computed. The ground is the median of a band 8 to 24 CSS px around the wolf, `#0e131d`; the wolf's body next to its eyes is as dark (`#10131f`). Contrast is the WCAG ratio. A computed colour is a palette colour put through the camera's colour grade (the game's own colour matrix: saturation -0.16, contrast +0.07), which gives the measured `#f92851` for the new red and the measured `#8b494c` for today's eye at 55%.

The fire is not in this frame: its main and darkest reds, `#e2533f` and `#a8302f` in the palette, are computed at full strength (`#e0604e`, `#a63a39`).

The new red is not brighter than today's eye at its brightest (4.8:1 against 6.0:1), and only a little brighter than today's at the middle of its pulse (4.2:1). What it adds is four times the area, a steady light and, for most players, a hue of its own.

Red-green colour blindness (about 1 in 12 men, in some degree) takes most of that hue away. Simulated in its full forms (Machado 2009; computed): with deuteranopia the new red becomes the fire's main red (a CIEDE2000 difference of 0.7); with deuteranopia and protanopia alike it stays close to the ambient pairs (5.1 and 5.5, the same hue within 3 degrees); and with protanopia it drops to 3.2:1 on the ground, below an ambient pair (3.4:1). For normal vision the difference is about 10.5 from both: clear side by side, less so on dots this small. So what sets the wolf apart from the ambient pairs for every player is its size (each eye 4 times the area of an ambient eye in 1 and 3, 9 times in 2), the glow in 3, and a steady pair that moves along its round, where the ambient pairs stand still, blink once and fade.

Why this red: the night's lights are orange-red (the fire and the ambient pairs, `#e2533f`, and the fire's darker `#a8302f`), gold and yellow (the lantern, sparks), pale green (fireflies) and blue (glowing mushrooms). The one red no light uses is crimson. The core palette has no bright crimson (its `#5e1c28` is the dark end of the red ramp), so the eyes use `#ff1744`, which the grade draws as `#f92851`. That red is off the 32-colour core palette, as the senses' glints are (`#ffe28c`, `#fffae6`), so each of the three is an exception to the style compass of 2026-10-09 in DECISIONS.md ("light that stays on the palette") and needs the owner's yes. The way that stays on the palette, not shown here: keep today's red and mute the ambient pairs within about 10 tiles of a round.

## The question

Which eyes: **1, 2 or 3**? And should the wolf's eyes **blink or pulse: yes or no**? All three stills are steady; today's eyes pulse, so a no drops that pulse.

Recommended default: **3, steady (no blink, no pulse).**

- 3: the glow is the one change that gives the pair a shape the ambient pairs do not have, a soft patch 24x16 CSS px around two 2x2 eyes, so telling the wolf apart does not rest on the red; that matters most to the colour-blind players above. Its costs: the inner ring fills the gap between the eyes (eye to gap 2.6:1, against 4.8:1 in 1), so the pair reads more as one patch with two bright points than as two eyes; the outer rings turn violet over the blue night; and if the eyes are to flare when the wolf sees the player, eyes that already glow need a bigger step for it.
- Steady: the ambient pairs fade in, blink once and fade out, so a wolf whose eyes blink or pulse acts more like them; a steady pair that moves is what the player learns to follow, and it stays at full strength all the time.

## After the pick

The pick and the blink-or-pulse answer go into DECISIONS.md with the picked still as its frame, together with the yes to an off-palette red, or the way that stays on the palette instead. The stealth client change then draws the wolf's eyes in that look; the renderer change that drew these stills was for the captures only and is not committed. The ambient pairs stay as they are unless the owner takes the way that stays on the palette. Until then the game keeps today's eyes.
