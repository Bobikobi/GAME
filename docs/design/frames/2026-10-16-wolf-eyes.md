# The bank wolf's eyes at night: three stills for the owner

At night the bank wolf of NEXT 12 shows only its eyes, and the player learns its round by them (G47). Today each eye is one game pixel of the orange-red the fire uses, breathing in and out, and the eye pairs that open at the forest edge at night are the same pixels in the same red. A play audit of the south bank found the wolf's eyes hard to pick out for that reason. These stills try the wolf's eyes in a red of their own, one difference each. Nothing here is in the game yet.

- `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-1-base.png`, `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-2-size.png`, `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-3-glow.png`: the three stills.
- `2026-10-16-wolf-eyes-crops.png`: the eyes of the three side by side, 48x32 CSS px around them enlarged six times, with an ambient pair from the same frame on the right.

## What each still tests

Sizes are in game pixels. On a phone the game draws one game pixel as 2x2 CSS px, so today's eyes are already 2x2 CSS px, and "2x2" here means 4x4 CSS px.

1. **base**: the eyes in the new red, 2x2 each, at full strength (today's breathe between 55% and 95%), no glow. Tests the red and the 2x2 eye.
2. **size**: as 1, with 3x3 eyes. Tests a bigger eye.
3. **glow**: as 1, plus a soft glow drawn the way the lantern's is: whole-pixel rings 1, 2 and 3 pixels out, the red added as light at 40%, 20% and 8%. Tests a glow.

Everything else is as it is today, the ambient pairs included. The eyes sit where today's do, ahead on the wolf's heading, turned on the east-west legs.

## The moment

One frame on a 390x844 phone at 21:59, the same in all three: the player on the tall grass at the south end of the bank with the lantern lit, the wolf on its round at (11, 27) coming south, six tiles north of the player and outside the lantern's light, and an ambient pair at the right edge. The game was paused on that frame and only the eyes were redrawn between the captures, so the stills match pixel for pixel outside the eyes. A second capture of still 1, taken last, matched the first exactly.

## The numbers

Measured on the stills. The ground is the median of a band 8 to 24 CSS px around the wolf, `#0e131d`; the wolf's body next to its eyes is as dark (`#10131f`). Contrast is the WCAG ratio. Hue distance is measured from the nearest other light of the night, the fire's darkest red (`#a8302f` in the palette, `#a63a39` as drawn, not in this frame), and from the ambient pairs.

Why this red: the night's lights are orange-red (the fire and the ambient pairs, `#e2533f`, and the fire's darker `#a8302f`), gold and yellow (the lantern, sparks), pale green (fireflies) and blue (glowing mushrooms). The one red no light uses is crimson. The core palette has no bright crimson (its `#5e1c28` is the dark end of the red ramp), so the eyes use `#ff1744`, off the core palette as the senses' glints are (`#ffe28c`, `#fffae6`). The camera's colour grade draws it as `#f92851`.

## The question

Which eyes: **1, 2 or 3**? And should the wolf's eyes **blink: yes or no**?

Recommended default: **3, no blink.**

- 3: it is the only one whose eyes glow in the dark, as NEXT 12 describes them, and the glow widens the pair to 24x16 CSS px while each eye keeps the 2x2 size.
- No blink: the ambient pairs already blink once each time they open, so a blinking wolf would act like them; a steady pair that moves is what the player learns to follow.
