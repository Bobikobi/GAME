# How the leading games split making into families - survey

Clean-room study, 21 Nov 2026. Ten games: the six required (Old School RuneScape, World of
Warcraft, Albion Online, New World, Final Fantasy XIV, The Elder Scrolls Online) plus four chosen
because each teaches something different: Guild Wars 2 (a hard limit of two active families),
Valheim (the world is the gate, not experience), Stardew Valley (a small tree with two-way
choices), Path of Exile 2 (the shape of a big tree with small and big nodes).

**Words used here.** *Family* = a profession or skill line (Tailoring, Smithing). *XP* = experience
points earned by doing the activity. *Tier* = a step of materials and recipes (bronze, iron, steel).
*Node* = one bonus you can pick on a skill tree. *Specialist* = a player who went deep in a family.

**How numbers are marked.** `[S7]` = I read it on the page listed under that number below (page
name and section). `(unchecked)` = from memory or from a search snippet whose page I could not open.
"Derived" = my own arithmetic on checked numbers.

## 1. Old School RuneScape

1. **Families.** 24 skills in four groups [S1]: production (Cooking, Crafting, Fletching, Herblore,
   Runecraft, Smithing), gathering (Farming, Fishing, Hunter, Mining, Woodcutting), utility
   (Agility, Construction, Firemaking, Sailing, Slayer, Thieving) and combat. The split follows the
   *activity and material*: Smithing turns metal bars into weapons, armour and tools [S5]; Fletching
   is wood into bows and arrows, Crafting is a catch-all for leather, jewellery, glass and pottery
   (unchecked).
2. **Size and cost.** Every skill runs 1-99 on one table: 13,034,431 XP to 99, and level 92 is
   almost exactly half (6,517,253) [S2]. XP keeps counting to 200,000,000 [S2]. Smithing's metal
   tiers open at 1 (bronze), 15 (iron), 30 (steel), 50 (mithril), 70 (adamant), 85 (rune) [S5].
   The curve is shared, the **hours are not**: Cooking gives up to 490,000 XP/h from level 68 [S3],
   Runecraft about 65,000-70,000 XP/h at its best group method and 77,121 XP/h at level 99 solo
   [S4]. Derived: roughly 27 h vs about 170-200 h for the same 13 M XP, a gap of about 7 to 1.
   Hours to a first useful piece: minutes in every skill (unchecked).
3. **Parity.** Only the XP table. It famously fails on time: Runecraft and Agility are the community's
   "slow 99s", Cooking and Fletching the fast ones (unchecked, third-party lists).
4. **What depth pays.** Higher levels unlock better materials and recipes, and lower failure (for
   example less burnt food) (unchecked). No specialist status, no edge outside the skill, no penalty.
5. **How many deep.** All of them, on one character. Trade happens because time is scarce, not
   because anything is locked.
6. **Leaning.** Gathering is its own family: Mining feeds Smithing [S5]. Sailing leans on three old
   skills: boat upgrades give Construction XP, deep sea trawling is a Fishing-Sailing hybrid, and
   keel parts need Smithing [S6].
7. **Late family.** Sailing, released 19 Nov 2025, the only skill exclusive to OSRS [S6]. It came in
   as a full 1-99 skill that *consumes* old skills' products, so it added demand to them instead of
   replacing them.
8. **World.** Sailing opens 27 islands with 56 docking points, some with exclusive monsters and
   training methods [S6]. Materials and training spots are tied to places across the map.

## 2. World of Warcraft (Classic and modern specialisations)

1. **Families.** Primary gathering: Herbalism, Mining, Skinning. Primary crafting: Alchemy,
   Blacksmithing, Engineering, Jewelcrafting, Leatherworking, Tailoring, plus Enchanting and
   Inscription. Secondary, open to everyone: Cooking, Fishing, Archaeology (First Aid removed in
   8.0.1) [S7]. The split follows the *material* (cloth, leather, metal) with some product
   families on top (Engineering gadgets, Jewelcrafting gems).
2. **Size and cost.** Classic: skill 1-300 in four equal brackets, Apprentice 1-75, Journeyman
   75-150, Expert 150-225, Artisan 225-300 [S8], identical for every profession. Modern: each
   expansion has its own skill line; The War Within and Midnight run 1-100 [S7]. Dragonflight
   Alchemy had 3 specialisation trees needing 510 knowledge points to fill [S9]. In The War Within
   both Blacksmithing and Tailoring get four specialisations opening at skill 25, 50, 60 and 75
   [S11]. Hours to the top: about one patch cycle of weekly play (unchecked).
3. **Parity.** Same skill brackets, same unlock levels for specialisations, and *knowledge points* -
   a currency earned weekly - as the shared measure. Where it failed: weekly income differs by
   profession - Engineering 20, Alchemy 23, Blacksmithing, Leatherworking and Tailoring 29,
   Inscription 31 [S10]. Derived: 1.55 to 1 between the slowest and fastest crafter. Older failure:
   each profession gave its own combat bonus (extra sockets, extra enchants); Blizzard tried to
   keep them even "with mixed success" and removed the direct combat benefits in 6.0 [S12].
4. **What depth pays.** Each knowledge point gives +1 skill on the recipes of its node. Milestones
   add Resourcefulness (a chance to use fewer materials), Multicraft (extra copies), Ingenuity,
   +10% to +15% crafting speed and, at 30 points, access to special finishing materials [S9].
   Higher skill means a higher quality rank. All of it works only inside the profession.
5. **How many deep.** Two primary professions per character [S7]. This made crafting orders and the
   auction house central; secondaries are free for all.
6. **Leaning.** Gathering is its own family, usually paired: Mining with Blacksmithing [S8]. Recipes
   borrow parts across professions (thread, gems, engineering parts) (unchecked detail).
7. **Late families.** Jewelcrafting and Inscription came in later expansions (unchecked). Each new
   expansion starts a fresh skill line at 1 [S7], so old lines are not devalued as levels, but
   their products are outgrown by new gear (unchecked).
8. **World.** Gathering nodes by zone; one-time knowledge treasures hidden in the world and weekly
   treasure drops [S10].

## 3. Albion Online (the Destiny Board)

1. **Families.** One board for everything: combat, gathering, refining, crafting, farming [S13].
   Crafting is split by *station and product*: Warrior's Forge (plate, swords, axes), Hunter's Lodge
   (leather, bows, spears), Mage's Tower (cloth, staffs), Toolmaker (unchecked) - then one node per
   item line inside each.
2. **Size and cost.** Tiers T1-T8; each item node levels to 100 (unchecked). From Tier 4 each level's
   fame is split: the first 20% must be earned by doing the activity, the other 80% can be paid with
   Learning Points [S13]. Months to master a whole station line (unchecked).
3. **Parity.** Nodes of the same kind cost the same fame. When new weapons arrived, the new nodes
   "require the same amount of Fame as a normal node" [S13].
4. **What depth pays.** Combat side: +0.2 item power per level for weapon and armour nodes, +0.1 for
   artifact nodes [S13]. Crafting side, per level of an artifact node: +0.15 focus cost efficiency
   and +0.375 quality bonus [S13]. *Focus* is a daily budget that raises material return and
   quality: 10,000 per day with the paid membership, at most 30,000 stored; the focus cost halves
   for every 10,000 efficiency, and a fully mastered line reaches 40,000, so 6.25% of base cost [S14].
   The edge is inside the family; there is no penalty outside.
5. **How many deep.** All of it: "It is possible to master all areas of the Destiny Board on one
   character" [S13]. In practice the daily focus budget makes players pick a few lines, so a large
   player market forms around specialists.
6. **Leaning.** Gathering, refining and crafting are separate lines; crafters buy refined
   materials. Each royal city gives a refining bonus to one resource (sources disagree on which
   city, unchecked).
7. **Late family.** New artifact weapons in Aug 2020: the old node was split into one node per item,
   its fame divided evenly, and levels converted so that item power came out "equal to or better
   than" before [S13]. The textbook way to add content without devaluing old progress.
8. **World.** Higher tiers live in more dangerous zones (unchecked); city bonuses pull refining to
   specific places.

## 4. New World (trade skills)

1. **Families.** 5 gathering (Mining, Skinning, Logging, Fishing, Harvesting), 5 refining (Smelting,
   Stonecutting, Woodworking, Tanning/Leatherworking, Weaving) and 7 crafting (Weaponsmithing,
   Armoring, Jewelcrafting, Engineering, Arcana, Cooking, Furnishing) [S15][S16]. Three layers:
   gathering and refining follow the *material*, crafting follows the *product*.
2. **Size and cost.** Launch cap 200 (unchecked); the Rise of the Angry Earth expansion raised the
   crafting cap from 200 to 250 together with maximum gear score 625 to 700 [S17]. The famous
   imbalance: Weaponsmithing 150-200 needed at least 865 Orichalcum round shields, about 75,000 XP
   per level and about 82,600 gold of bought materials, because metal was scarce [S18].
3. **Parity.** Same cap for all, same structure. It failed on *shared inputs*: several crafts used
   the same metal, so the craft with the most metal per XP became the most expensive [S18].
4. **What depth pays.** Higher level raises the minimum gear score of crafted items and the chance
   of extra perks; trade-skill clothing and house trophies add bonuses (unchecked).
5. **How many deep.** "You can maximize every single Trade Skill on one character" [S16]; players are
   "free to master as many or as few ... Trade Skills" as they like [S15]. Trade comes from cost.
6. **Leaning.** Refining sits between gathering and crafting; crafted items mix refined materials
   from several refining skills; special foods boost gathering and crafting (unchecked).
7. **Late tier.** The 250 cap was raised for all crafting at once [S17]. New rare gathered items feed
   refining stations, and research items were limited to 3 crafts per day per item [S17].
8. **World.** Stations live in settlements and are upgraded by town projects; some resources are
   regional (unchecked).

## 5. Final Fantasy XIV (crafters and gatherers)

1. **Families.** 8 crafters: Carpenter (wood, bows, staves), Blacksmith (weapons), Armorer (metal
   armour), Goldsmith (accessories), Leatherworker (leather armour), Weaver (cloth armour),
   Alchemist (potions, inks, books), Culinarian (food) [S19]. 3 gatherers: Miner, Botanist, Fisher
   [S20]. The split follows the *material*, with metal split again into weapons and armour.
2. **Size and cost.** Every class has the same cap (currently levelled to 100) [S19] and the same
   curve; all are the same size. Hours per class to the cap: tens of hours (unchecked).
3. **Parity.** Same cap, same curve, shared crafting stats (Craftsmanship, Control, CP) and shared
   gear for all crafters except tools [S19]. Each expansion raises every cap together (unchecked).
4. **What depth pays.** Levels unlock recipes. The *specialist* system: up to 3 crafters at once,
   each getting +20 Craftsmanship, +20 Control, +15 CP and three extra actions (at levels 55, 86, 96)
   that spend a consumable; specialist-only recipes are **cosmetic only** and rotate out [S21].
   Unlocked at level 55 [S21]. So the specialist edge is small by design.
5. **How many deep.** All classes on one character; the guide recommends levelling them together
   "as they feed each other with intermediate materials" [S22]. High-quality endgame items sell well.
6. **Leaning.** Gatherers are separate classes: Miner feeds Blacksmith, Armorer, Goldsmith; Botanist
   feeds Carpenter, Weaver, Culinarian, Alchemist [S20]. The heavy web of intermediate parts is the
   reason players feel they must level everything [S22].
7. **Late additions.** No new crafter in the game's life (unchecked); Fisher gained spearfishing in
   Stormblood (4.0) [S20].
8. **World.** Timed "unspoiled" nodes from level 46 appear only at set in-game hours and last 2
   in-game hours [S20], sending gatherers across zones on a clock.

## 6. The Elder Scrolls Online (crafting lines and trait research)

1. **Families.** 7 [S23]: Alchemy, Blacksmithing (heavy armour and metal weapons), **Clothing (light
   cloth armour and medium leather armour)**, Enchanting, Jewelry Crafting, Provisioning (food and
   drink), Woodworking (staves, bows, shields). The split mixes station and material; Clothing
   proves that cloth and leather can live in one family.
2. **Size and cost.** Ranks 1-50; total XP ("inspiration") to rank 50 is 3,667,895 and is
   "identical across all seven professions" [S23]. Skill points to max a line differ: Jewelry 18,
   Alchemy 21, Enchanting 23, Provisioning 25, Blacksmithing, Clothing and Woodworking 26 (165 in
   all) [S23]. Trait research: the first trait on an item takes 6 h and each next one doubles;
   9 traits take 18 weeks 4 days per item, 255 weeks for all Blacksmithing items with one slot,
   55 weeks 4 days with the top passive (3 slots, 10-day cap) [S24][S25].
3. **Parity.** Identical XP; refining odds "the same between all professions" [S23]; one research
   structure for all. The gap that remains: skill points (18 vs 26, derived 1.44 to 1).
4. **What depth pays.** Higher ranks open higher material tiers [S25]; extraction passives raise the
   chance of upgrade materials by about 12.5% per level [S23]; research passives add parallel slots
   and cut time [S24]. Known traits are the real specialist gate: you can only put a trait you
   researched on that item type [S25].
5. **How many deep.** All seven on one character, but the 165 skill points compete with combat
   skills [S23]; many players keep a dedicated crafting character (unchecked).
6. **Leaning.** Refining is inside each family [S23]; Enchanting's glyphs go onto everyone else's
   items. Gathering is not a family: anyone harvests (unchecked).
7. **Late family.** Jewelry Crafting came with the Summerset chapter and is learned only in Alinor
   [S23]. It plugged into the same curve but with the fewest skill points (18) [S23].
8. **World.** Special "set stations" sit at crafting sites in the wild, three per zone in the early
   zones [S26]; each crafts one item set. Crafting styles are found as books in the world
   (unchecked).

## 7. Guild Wars 2 (two active disciplines)

1. **Families.** 9 disciplines [S27]: Armorsmith (heavy), Leatherworker (medium), Tailor (light),
   Weaponsmith (melee and shields), Huntsman (ranged, torches, warhorns), Artificer (magic weapons,
   potions), Jeweler (trinkets), Chef (food, dyes), Scribe (guild items); plus Handiworker (home
   decorations). The split follows the *product* - armour by weight class, weapons by kind.
2. **Size and cost.** Max level 500, but Jeweler and Scribe stop at 400 [S27]. Materials run in
   tiers 1-7 and **every tier has a cloth, a leather, a metal and a wood** [S27]. Each crafting
   level gives 1% to 4% of a character level, so crafting also levels the character [S27]. Hours
   to 400: a few hours if materials are bought (unchecked).
3. **Parity.** One tier table across all materials; the three armour weights mirror each other.
   The visible gap: 400 vs 500 caps.
4. **What depth pays.** Crafted items "always have comparable attributes" to loot of the same
   level and rarity [S27]: crafting is a second road to gear, not a better one. The real edge is
   the top-tier and legendary parts (unchecked).
5. **How many deep.** Two active at once, four with paid licences; switching costs 10 copper per
   level already reached, 50 silver to return to a maxed one; progress is always kept [S27]. The
   newest discipline, Handiworker, does not count against the limit and is shared account-wide [S27].
6. **Leaning.** Gathering is no family at all: anyone with the right tool gathers, and the yield
   depends "only on the node and the type of tool" [S28]. Disciplines share intermediate parts
   such as inscriptions [S27].
7. **Late tier.** The 500 cap came later for top-tier gear (unchecked); Scribe came later at 400
   (unchecked); Handiworker came outside the two-slot limit [S27].
8. **World.** Nodes follow terrain logic - ore in rocky areas, wood in forests, vegetables on farms
   [S28]; stations in cities and some zones; a discovery panel lets players find recipes by
   trying combinations [S27].

## 8. Valheim (the world is the gate)

1. **Families.** None in the usual sense: one Crafting skill and one Cooking skill, plus Farming,
   Fishing and so on [S29]. The real split is by *station*: Workbench, Forge and others.
2. **Size and cost.** Skills run 1-100 [S29]. Crafting at max gives -60% crafting time and a 25%
   chance of an extra item; Cooking the same pattern for food [S29]. Death lowers skills [S29].
   Workbench upgrades to level 5, Forge to level 7, each step built from new materials [S29].
3. **Parity.** Not by XP: by distance. Meadows 0-5,100 m from the centre, Black Forest 500-8,000 m,
   Plains 2,900-8,000 m, Mistlands 5,900-10,000 m [S29 Biomes]. Everyone climbs the same ladder.
4. **What depth pays.** Speed and extra output only. Quality comes from station level, which lets
   items be upgraded more times [S29 Workbench].
5. **How many deep.** Everyone does everything; trade is sharing within a group.
6. **Leaning.** Stations borrow other materials: the Tanning Rack needs hides, the Forge Bellows
   needs deer hide and chain [S29]. The Forge also makes wolf and padded armour [S29 Forge] - metal
   and hide pieces meet at one station.
7. **Late content.** Only 5 of 9 biomes had content at launch in Feb 2021 [S29]; each new biome added
   rows to existing stations and outgrew older gear (unchecked).
8. **World.** The strongest example: the Forge itself costs 6 copper [S29], copper lives in the
   Black Forest [S29], and each next metal needs the next biome. Making is how exploring pays.

## 9. Stardew Valley (a small tree with two-way choices)

1. **Families.** 5 skills: Farming, Mining, Foraging, Fishing, Combat [S30]. No making skill: crafting
   and cooking recipes are *rewarded by* the gathering skills' levels [S30].
2. **Size and cost.** 10 levels, one table for all, 15,000 XP to level 10 [S30]. A full farming
   year or so to max (unchecked).
3. **Parity.** Same table, same shape: at levels 5 and 10 every skill offers a choice of one of two
   *professions* [S30].
4. **What depth pays.** Each level lowers the energy a tool costs by 0.1 [S30]; professions give
   benefits such as higher sell prices [S30].
5. **How many deep.** All five. Small game, solo or co-op.
6. **Leaning.** Gathering *is* the family; making hangs off it.
7. **Late content.** A later update added mastery past level 10 (unchecked).
8. **World.** Each skill is practised in its own place: the mines, the forest, the shore (unchecked).

## 10. Path of Exile 2 (shape of a large tree, not crafting)

1. **Shape.** Four node kinds: travel nodes (small attribute picks), minor passives, notable passives
   and keystones [S31]. Keystones change a rule and usually carry a cost (unchecked).
2. **Size.** Well over a thousand nodes (unchecked); one point per level plus quest points (unchecked);
   about 24 extra points for weapon-set nodes (unchecked).
3. **Respec.** Paid in gold; the price depends on level and how deep the node sits; attribute nodes
   refund at half price [S31].
4. **Lesson.** Small nodes are cheap filler, notables are the reason to walk a path, keystones are
   identity. A crafting tree can borrow the shape at a far smaller size.

## What the leaders agree on

- One shared XP curve for every family (OSRS, ESO, FFXIV, Stardew); a new cap is raised for all at once (New World, FFXIV).
- The same number of tiers per family, and each tier offers every material (GW2's tier table).
- Depth pays in efficiency - speed, fewer materials, extra copies, better quality chance - not in raw combat power; WoW removed profession combat perks [S12].
- The edge stays inside its family; nobody is punished for not being a specialist.
- What really separates specialists is a scarce budget: daily focus (Albion), weekly knowledge (WoW), research time (ESO), active slots (GW2, FFXIV).
- A late family plugs into the same curve and *uses* older families' products (OSRS Sailing, ESO Jewelry, Albion's split nodes).

## Where they differ

- How many deep: all (OSRS, New World, ESO, Albion), three (FFXIV specialists), two (WoW, GW2).
- Split axis: material (WoW, FFXIV), product (GW2, New World crafting), station or world (Valheim), mixed (ESO Clothing holds cloth and leather).
- What gates progress: XP alone (OSRS), XP plus points (WoW, ESO), XP plus a daily budget (Albion), world distance (Valheim).
- Real cost parity: same XP but up to about 7 to 1 in hours (OSRS, derived), or a shared material pricing one craft out (New World [S18]).
- Interdependence: a heavy web (FFXIV), light shared parts (GW2), none (Stardew).
- Specialist reward: exclusive power (old WoW perks, Albion item power), cosmetic only (FFXIV), speed only (Valheim).
- Gathering: its own family (OSRS, WoW, FFXIV, New World) versus open to anyone with a tool (GW2, Valheim, ESO).
