# The hunt families: one body, one way of hunting, one ladder of goods per family (2026-10-30)

**Status:** Proposed 30.10, written for the owner. Nothing here is built but the coat format, which is in #433. It builds on [HUNTING.md](HUNTING.md) (cited "HUNTING n"), [BESTIARY.md](BESTIARY.md) ("BESTIARY n"), and two pages of #432 that are not merged yet, BESTIARY_LANDS.md ("LANDS n") and MATERIALS.md ("MATERIALS n"), and on [CREATURE_BEHAVIOUR.md](CREATURE_BEHAVIOUR.md) ("BEHAVIOUR n"), proposed beside it. Every number below is quoted from those pages with its section; a number, a kind, a tool or an act this page adds is marked proposed. The game as it stands is read as a first version: where a better hunt needs a new tool or a changed rule, the page proposes it (section 3a) and names the rule it meets with the change (section 9a), instead of shrinking the hunt to fit. Section 3.1 also carries the line home, the found den and the flush (its tool the smoke bundle, T2f on the ladder); section 6 adds an ambient member, the gull, and 6.1 the group behaviours that BEHAVIOUR 5.1's groups make possible.

The ask behind it, 30.10: many more creatures over time, organised in families, each family with a hunting style of its own, a difficulty fitted to it, and goods to gather from it.

**Why families.** A family is one body and one rig, so a new member is a look, a scale and a row of numbers, not a new model: the clip tool renders a body's own actions through its native target (ANIMATION 5.4), and a family's members share that body under another ramp, as MATERIALS 6 already does for the goods ("the family changes the coat ramp on one drawing, not the drawing"). A family is one hunting style, so a player who learns the family learns its members: the stalk that takes a roe deer takes a white hart, at a longer sight. And the difficulty climbs inside the family, across the lands and the distance from the town (d0, d1, d2; G64: `d` is the steps from the town divided by 64), with the four levers LANDS 1 names and never a hit-point number: a longer take, a higher way on the tool ladder, a higher danger, a thinner spawn.

What it does not reopen: the ten states, the noticing count and the noise table (HUNTING 2 to 4), except the proposed amendments section 9a lists; the six tools by tier (HUNTING 5), to which section 3a adds proposed tools, acts and companions on the same ladder; the five goods and the carcass (HUNTING 6); the caps (HUNTING 8 and 9); the rating's rules (HUNTING 10); G34 (a predator on its round is a hazard escaped by a run) and G35 (no fighting in the demo), so every `fight` way below waits for the combat stream and a defender only bluffs until then (HUNTING 11, question 1).

## 1. The words

- **Family:** a set of kinds that share one body, one hunting style and one shape of drops. A kind belongs to exactly one family.
- **Style:** the family's own combination of a tool, a way, a place and a temperament, which no other family shares whole (single tools such as the snare, the pit and the sling recur across families). A style is built from what exists or is designed, the tools T0 to T5 (HUNTING 5), the ways `stalk`, `snare`, `lure`, `fish`, `fight` (LANDS 1), the noticing count (HUNTING 3), and the temperament fields `flight`, `sleeps`, `alarm`, `looks_back`, `drinks`, `carrion` and the fire and water codes (BEHAVIOUR 1 and 5), and from the new tools, acts, gear, companions and skill nodes this page proposes (section 3a), each marked proposed where it enters. Every family has a primary style and one alternative (section 3).
- **Tell:** what the player reads on screen before the animal acts (BESTIARY 2, rule 9).
- **Mark:** the one good only that family gives, where the sources name one (section 5).
- Senses are sight / ear / smell. The ear is written as LANDS and BESTIARY write it (keen +2, normal +1, dull 0); HUNTING 3 writes the ear as -2, 0 or +2, a disagreement section 9 lists.

## 3. The hunting styles

A primary style and one alternative a family. Each names the tell, the act, what fails, and the source rows. What exists or is designed is quoted with its section; a tool, act or number of section 3a is marked proposed where it first enters a family. A family's alternative is never a second way to the same tile with the same tool: it changes the place, the hour or the tool, so a player who has learned the primary still has something to learn.

### 3.1 Burrowers: the snare on the line home

- **Style.** A burrower's flight ends at its own anchor and it is gone into it for 8 steps, then WANDER (`flight` B, BEHAVIOUR 1), so its run is the one predictable run in the game: from where it grazes back to its anchor. For the hare the anchor is its burrow at the meadow's edge (`sleeps` den, BEHAVIOUR 3 and 5: "snared between its burrow and the meadow"); for the rat it is its roof (`sleeps` roof, BEHAVIOUR 5). The snare (T1) sits on that line, on the trail sign between the burrow and the meadow (HUNTING 5: "small prey on a trail sign"), and the hare finds it on its way back. The line is now read, not guessed: the hare's route from its burrow to its grazing ground carries its prints and signs for every player (proposed, 3a, tracking), so the snare goes where the prints are thickest. Putting the hare up on purpose so it runs into the snare is proposed: it needs a snare that catches a fleeing kind crossing its tile, a new rule for HUNTING 5 (section 9a). The unaware one can also be stoned (T0, range 3) while its head is down.
- **Alternative (proposed): the flush at the bolt hole.** For the kinds that sleep in a den: the hare (`sleeps` den, BEHAVIOUR 5) and the proposed marmot and badger of section 6. Once a player has found a den (the found den, below), the smoke bundle (3a, T2f) set on its mouth bolts the animal inside out of the den's bolt hole, its second mouth 8 to 10 tiles away, into a purse net (3a) or a snare set there; the take is the trap's, a fade at the bolt hole (the flush, below). The bundle is the default. If the owner opens the companions (section 10, question 9), the ferret (3a) sent into the den is the companion alternative, silent where the bundle is heard (section 10, question 13). By day the hare has a second alternative, the dog (3a), which points it while it grazes or sits up: it is awake and unaware, so the throw net (3a, range 3) may fall on it, but only head down (GRAZE, sight 6 - 2 = 4) and from tall grass (2 off): 2, inside the net's 3. The dog finds the hare; the stalk is still the player's. The rat sleeps under a roof with no second mouth: its alternative is the purse net on the entry tile of its roof anchor, and a walk through the hall that scatters the group so that each runs home (its `flight` B ends at its anchor, BEHAVIOUR 1) and the one that crosses the net is taken (a trap takes an aware kind, 9a).
- **Tell.** The sit-up, the hare's only one: it has no alarm and no voice (BEHAVIOUR 5, "the hare has no voice and no water"). The rat's tell is the scatter, "the hall's first tell" (LANDS 3.2). At the den: a puff of earth at the bolt hole one step before the bolt (proposed, a two-pose cue).
- **Fails.** A snare off the line catches nothing; a hare that reaches its burrow stays in while a player is near and comes out 8 steps after (inside, while watched, below); a throw net cast at a hare from open ground, where its 4 sees the thrower. A crow can take what the snare holds (HUNTING 5), so a snare left past a day may be empty. A flush with no trap on the bolt hole sends the animal running away from the den with nothing on it, and its den is left alone for 60 steps (HUNTING 2, FLEE); a player standing within 2 of the bolt hole sends it out by the mouth, past the flusher; a den that is empty, its burrower out grazing: the bundle is not used up and nothing stirs; a den not found: no flush is offered on it.
- **The line home.** A burrower's FLEE ends at its anchor (BEHAVIOUR 1, `flight` B), so a player who follows it stands at its den in the end. The reference: Don't Starve's rabbit "runs to its hole at dusk or when it witnesses a rabbit die" (research-hunting, the catalogue). The player follows two things, both proposed on state that exists. First, the run itself: a hare flees at the run and the player runs at the same `walk_step_ms / gait.run_div` (HUNTING 2), so the gap at the start is the gap at the end, and a hare put up from 6 tiles (its sight, BESTIARY 1) ends 6 tiles ahead, inside the player's view. Second, its prints (3a, tracking): where the ground holds prints (meadow, grove, riverbank and path tiles), the fleeing hare's fresh-print ring of its last 16 tiles points the way it went, and its route's prints and signs run from its grazing ground to its den; on ruin, town, interior and water there are no prints and the follow is by sight alone. The line the player sees in the grass is the drawing of those prints, not a separate trail mark (section 9a). It is never a blood trail: the content ladder's "hurt animal's trail" stays at no rung by default (LADDER 2; HUNTING 10 and 11, question 5), so the line home is never read from blood.
- **The found den.** When a burrower ends FLEE at its anchor while a player has it in sight (HUNTING 3's line test at that think step), or when a player stands next to the den's sign at the end of its route's prints (3a, tracking), the den is **found** for that player: the mouth drawn on the anchor's tile, and its **bolt hole**, a second tile 8 to 10 tiles from the mouth on the burrower's walkable habitat, chosen once per anchor by the server when the anchor is laid and stored on it. The distance is HUNTING 3's measure, the squared distance: 64 <= d2 <= 100. The choice is deterministic: list the burrower's habitat tiles in that ring that are not solid and not water and that the burrower's ground flee map reaches from the mouth, in ring order (nearest first, each ring clockwise from north), start at `draw(seed, "bolt:<anchor id>", 0) mod` the list's length, and take that tile; a solid tile, a water tile or an off-habitat tile is never in the list, so no draw lands on one, and the hare can always leave by it and a player can always stand on it to set the net. (On the authored valley `meadow` holds solid rock tiles and `grove` solid forest tiles, `content/core/map.json`, which is why the tag alone is not enough.) Its own entity string keeps it apart from every other draw on the anchor (BEHAVIOUR 5.1.2, the size draw). The distance keeps a flusher on a tile next to the mouth, diagonal included, more than 6 from the bolt hole, beyond a hare's sight of 6 (HUNTING 3: seen when d2 <= sight x sight), and never within 2 of it. Found dens are remembered **per player**: a list of anchor ids on the player's state, one write at the find. Bound: at most the burrowers' anchors of the world; on the first map, at the cap of 12 per 1000 over an estimated 10,000 meadow and forest tiles of the 96 x 96 floor and the highland (FIRST_MAP), about 120 ids at the very most, an estimate. Section 10, question 14 asks per player against shared.
- **The den stays put.** A den anchor never moves: BEHAVIOUR 2's crowd row moves a herd's anchor 8 tiles at 3 players and already exempts "a hunter's den"; this extends the exemption to every anchor whose `sleeps` is `den`, and a crowd sends a burrower to ground there instead (SLEEP at the anchor, senses at a third, as the crowd row's six-player line). **proposed.**
- **Inside, while watched.** BEHAVIOUR 1's "gone into it 8 steps, then WANDER" would bring a hare back out under the player's feet and make the den a short wait, not a place. Proposed amendment (section 9a): a burrower in its den stays in while any player stands within its ear's reach of the mouth for a walk (`noise_walk` 5 + its ear, HUNTING 3 and 4: 7 tiles for the hare's +2), and comes out at WANDER 8 steps after the last such player has gone. The den is where the hunt pauses and the next tier begins.
- **The flush.** A flush is an act, `flush`, on a found den's mouth (range 1: the player stands on a tile next to the mouth) while the burrower is inside, asleep in its hours (the hare's night, 20-05) or gone in after a flight and kept in while watched. At its next think step (at most 8 ticks, HUNTING 1) it leaves the den at the run by the bolt hole, out within 2 steps, or by the mouth when a player stands within 2 of the bolt hole, and runs FLEE from the flusher. A flushed burrower's FLEE runs the ground distance map away from the den and does not end at its anchor; it RETURNs after the 60 steps (HUNTING 2, FLEE; section 9a, the flushed burrower row). A purse net or a snare on the bolt hole takes it as it crosses, a trap's live take (section 9a, the snare and passive traps rows), at the trap's grade and seconds (HUNTING 5 and 9). The flush makes no harm and no take of its own. Its tool, its rung and its numbers are one rule, the smoke bundle row of 3a, T2f on the ladder (below). **proposed.**
- **The rating's side.** The flush harms nothing: no smoke reaches the animal in the state, it bolts at its next think step, the den is never sealed, burned or dug, and the den is the same afterward (HUNTING 10: no suffering; LADDER 2: the take is a fade at both rungs). A flushed animal that escapes is unharmed and back at its den after 60 steps.
- **Sources.** HUNTING 2, 3, 4, 5, 9 and 10; LANDS 2 (hare: stalk, snare; hours 05-20) and 3.2 (rat: snare, stalk); BEHAVIOUR 1 (`flight` B), 2 (the crowd row, the carried light), 3 (the meadow), 5 (`sleeps` den and roof) and 5.1.2 (keyed draws); G31, G34, G45, G48; LADDER 2 (the hurt animal's trail); FIRST_MAP; SETTLEMENT 3.7 (companions); research-hunting, the catalogue (Don't Starve's rabbit hole); the core's `recipes/reed-cord.json`, `resources/reed-bank.json` and `wild-herbs.json`; 3a (tracking, smoke bundle, ferret, purse net, dog, throw net, passive traps).

### 3.2 Deer: the stalk at the water at dawn

- **Style.** Deer drink (`drinks` yes) and walk to water at dawn and at dusk (BEHAVIOUR 4, proposed), so the hunt is laid at the bank at first light. The player moves only while the head is down and freezes when it lifts: "a player who moves only while the head is down, and freezes when it lifts, is never noticed at the outer half" (HUNTING 3). The shot is the bow (T4, range 8) on an unaware animal, or the spear (T3, throw 4) closer; a snare on a trail is the patient way (LANDS 2 and 3.1). The numbers make the head-down beat the moment: the roe's sight is 8, 6 while it grazes (GRAZE is "sight 2 shorter", HUNTING 2), so the bow's 8 reaches it only head-down or from tall grass (2 off every sight, HUNTING 3); the hart's 9 becomes 7 the same way. Three proposed tools make the stalk richer (3a): the wind, since the roe smells at 6 on its upwind side, so the stalk comes from downwind; the spyglass, which reads head-up or head-down and the animal's quality from 16 tiles, so the player picks the deer and the beat before entering its sight; and the broad arrow, the bow's arrow for a medium or large kind at full quality.
- **Alternative (proposed): the call from the tree perch at the grove's edge.** A bone deer call (T3, 3a) is a noise of 6 the deer family answers: a roe that hears it (6 + 2 = 8 tiles) and has not sensed the player walks to 5 tiles from the call's tile and stands there in IDLE. From a tree perch (3a: 3 off a ground kind's sight) the roe's 8 is 5, the tile it stops on: it stands at the edge of its own sight, in the outer half, where the noticing count needs 3 steps in a row (HUNTING 3), so the bow's 8 is loosed inside those steps or while its head is down. Smell is the other half of the lesson: the perch halves the roe's smell on its upwind side (6 is 3), so at 5 tiles it smells nothing; a caller on the ground must call from downwind of the stop tile or in scent cover (3a). The hart answers too, but its 9 from the perch is 6, past the stop tile: it sees the caller unless its head goes down (GRAZE, 4), so the hart's call is a head-down shot, the same lesson one rung up. An animal that hears a second call within 20 think steps, from any player, goes to ALERT: in theHunter, too, it is the animal that grows wary of a call repeated too soon (3a, calls).
- **Tell.** The head up for two steps of GRAZE at full sight (HUNTING 2); for the hart, "head up for two steps before it goes, and the whole glade lifts with it" (LANDS 3.1). At the call: the ears turned to it and one step toward it (proposed, the listen pose of theHunter's per-sense alerts).
- **The look-back.** At the end of FLEE a deer takes two steps of ALERT at sight + 2, facing the player (BEHAVIOUR 1). For those two steps the roe notices at 10 tiles, so any player within the bow's 8 is already seen: the look-back is no second shot, it shows the player where the deer settles, and the stalk starts again from there after the 60 steps it keeps away from the tile it fled (HUNTING 2, FLEE).
- **Fails.** A move during the head-up; a walk inside its ear (walk 5 + 2 = 7 tiles); the herd: every member within 8 tiles goes to ALERT with the one that saw (HUNTING 2); an approach from upwind inside 6 tiles (proposed, the wind); a call from the ground upwind of the stop tile without scent cover; a second call the same deer hears too soon; a shot held past the 3 steps of the outer-half count.
- **Sources.** HUNTING 2, 3, 5; BESTIARY 1 (roe: "snare; bow or spear on an unaware animal"); LANDS 2 and 3.1; BEHAVIOUR 1, 4, 5; 3a (wind, spyglass, broad arrow, deer call, tree perch, scent cover).

### 3.3 Wild goats: the bow from below the ledge

- **Style.** The goat stands on a high tile, hears little (dull, 0) and sees far (8) (BESTIARY 1). So the approach is the opposite of the deer's: the player may walk (noise 5, heard at 5 tiles) but must stay out of the line from the ledge, coming from below, and shoots the bow from there (BESTIARY 1: "bow from below; snare at a ledge path"). The goat flees upward (`flight` C), so the snare waits on the ledge path it climbs. The spyglass (proposed, 3a) finds the high tile and the herd's watcher from 16 tiles, below the slope, before the climb.
- **Alternative (proposed): the salt lick under the hide.** A salt lick (a mineral find from digging, proposed, 3a bait) placed on a ledge path tile inside the herd's roam circle becomes its once-a-day target at dawn, as water is a drinker's (BEHAVIOUR 4), for 5 dawns; a brush hide (3a: 2 off every sight) 7 or 8 tiles from it (the goat's 8 is 6 from the hide) and the bow's 8 take the goat at the lick. It turns the climb into a wait, which suits a kind that hears nothing and sees everything.
- **Tell.** The goat turns on its high tile to face the source (ALERT, HUNTING 2); a herd of 5 to 12 lifts together (BEHAVIOUR 5, alarm 3).
- **Fails.** Standing in its line of sight on open slope; a snare below the ledge rather than on the climb; a hide built inside 6 tiles of the lick; a lick outside the herd's roam circle, which draws nothing.
- **Sources.** BESTIARY 1; LANDS 2 (stalk, bow from below, snare); BEHAVIOUR 4 and 5; 3a (spyglass, salt lick, brush hide).

### 3.4 Pigs: the pit and the charge in a line

- **Style.** The boar is a defender (danger 1): it "holds its ground facing the player; if the player stays within 2 tiles for 2 steps, or it is cornered or hit, it charges up to 5 tiles in a line and rests 6 steps" (HUNTING 2, DEFEND). Its ways are `fight` and `lure`, a pit with bait (LANDS 2); its gate is "spear from a ledge; pit trap with bait; never head on" (BESTIARY 1). So the boar is hunted by its hours and its charge: the pit (T5) is dug and baited while the boar sleeps in its den (09-19, BEHAVIOUR 4) and found after its evening or morning window (19-23, 05-09), and once the fight exists the charge is read and side-stepped, because it runs in a line and then rests. Proposed (3a): the bait is roots (the boar's bait type), a scent bait that draws a boar standing on its downwind side from 8 tiles (its smell), so the root bait is laid on a day the wind carries its scent from the pit to the boar's feeding ground: the player reads the day's arrow and baits the pit that sits upwind of the feeding ground that day. The wind is drawn each day and roots keep 3 game days, so a bait laid under the wrong wind draws nothing that evening and may draw on a later day of its three when the wind turns. The pit cover (3a) hides the pit from the boar's sight of 4, since a kind that sees an open pit drops a path through it.
- **Alternative (proposed): the drive hedge and the pit in its gap.** Instead of baiting a pit by the den, the player reads the boar's route between its den and its feeding ground by its signs (3a, tracking: its rooting and a wallow, signs that last a game day) and builds a drive hedge (3a, T3) across it, a line of brush with one gap, so the boar's evening walk is turned through the gap, where a covered pit with no bait (9a) takes it, resolved when checked (3a, passive traps). It changes the place and the tool: the lesson is the route and the shape of the ground, not the wind. Once the fight exists, a second alternative opens: the spear (T3, throw 4) from a tree perch (3a), where the boar's 4 is 1; a charge that ends on the perch's tile shakes the player down beside it (3a), so the charge is still read and side-stepped, BESTIARY 1's "spear from a ledge" given a place in the wood and not a safe spot.
- **Tell.** The low head held toward the player; until the fight it only bluffs, ALERT and its pose (HUNTING 2 and 11, question 1).
- **Fails.** Standing head-on within 2 tiles; digging in its waking hours (the dig is a noise of 5, HUNTING 4); an open pit it can see; a root bait laid under a wind that carries it away from the feeding ground; a hedge whose gap is off the route; before the fight there is no contact and no take but the pit.
- **Sources.** HUNTING 2, 4, 5, 11; BESTIARY 1; LANDS 2; 3a (root bait, wind, pit cover, tracking, drive hedge, passive traps, tree perch).

### 3.5 Canids: the laid bait at a dead end

- **Style.** Every canid comes to carrion (`carrion` yes for all four, BEHAVIOUR 5), and every canid's lure is a laid bait: a fowl's leavings for the silver fox, a bone for the shade fox, bait after the fight for the wolf, a pit after the fight for the hound (LANDS 2, 3.1, 3.2). The bait goes where the fox already goes, "the trail's dead ends" (LANDS 3.1), with a snare at it for a fox; the wolf's bait and the hound's pit wait for the fight. The player leaves the bait and returns, as with any snare (HUNTING 5). The fox's nose is the family's sense (smell 8 to 9), so the proposed wind (3a) is the family's real lesson: a bait laid where the wind carries it into the trail draws a fox standing downwind of it from 8 tiles (its smell, 3a bait), and the player who waits must be downwind of the fox too, or in scent cover (3a), since a fox smells an upwind player at 8. No built cover answers the fox's nose: its lesson stays the wind.
- **Alternative (proposed): the squeak call.** A reed squeak call (T1, 3a), a mouse's squeak, is a noise of 6 that the foxes answer: a fox that hears it (6 + 2 = 8) and has not sensed the player comes to 5 tiles from the call. From tall grass or a brush hide (2 off, 3a) the fox's 7 is 5, the tile it stops on: at the edge of its sight, in the outer half, the noticing count needs 3 steps (HUNTING 3), and the sling (T2, 6), which takes a small kind at full quality, must fly inside them. The caller is downwind of the stop tile or in scent cover, since the fox smells an upwind player at 8 and a hide takes nothing off smell. The real practice is old ("predator calling"); theHunter carries predator callers. Hunters (the wolf, and the hound as a defender) are never called (section 9a): a call that brings a hunter to the player before the fight would summon a hazard.
- **Tell.** The look-back: the fox "sits and looks at the player before it goes" (LANDS 3.1), the shade fox "looks back from the edge of the lamp's light" (LANDS 3.2); the wolf holds at a fire's edge facing in (BEHAVIOUR 2, fire). Proposed, at the call: the fox stops and lifts its nose, the smell pose, before it commits.
- **Fails.** A bait in the open meadow, where no canid ranges; a player upwind of the fox within 8 (proposed); a call from upwind of the stop tile without scent cover; a wolf baited before the fight, which LANDS 2 puts after it (HUNTING 8 reads "with the fight, or the pit trap", section 9); a hound pitted before the fight, since a defender only bluffs until then (HUNTING 11, question 1; LANDS 3.2).
- **Sources.** HUNTING 3, 5, 8 and 11; LANDS 2, 3.1, 3.2; BEHAVIOUR 2 and 5; 3a (wind, bait, scent cover, brush hide, squeak call).

### 3.6 Cats: the baited snare set while it sleeps

- **Style.** A cat never comes to a carcass (`carrion` no) and has no voice (alarm 0); it climbs and holds still (BEHAVIOUR 5). Its way is a baited snare (LANDS 2: wildcat "lure (a baited snare)"; LANDS 3.1: lynx, the same after the fight), set on its own trail through cover, the wood's rocks and fallen trunks or the grove, by day while it sleeps (hours 20-05 and 19-07), and checked once a game day (HUNTING 5). Proposed (3a): since a cat refuses a carcass, its bait is a scent herb (catmint, a proposed herb item, the cats' own bait type), which draws a cat and no canid or crow; and its trail is found by tracking, the prints in soft ground and a scratched trunk, a sign that lasts a game day.
- **Alternative (proposed): the box trap at the bed, set at night.** The prints and a scratched trunk (3a, tracking) lead to the bed sign (flattened ground in cover); a box trap (3a; BESTIARY 1's own "baited snare or box trap" for the wildcat), baited with catmint, is set on the bed's approach at night while the cat is out on its round, and takes it when it comes home at dawn, resolved when checked (3a, passive traps). It changes the hour and the tool: the primary snare is set by day on the trail while the cat sleeps; the box is set at night at the bed while it hunts. A trap is no contact, so the wildcat's box follows its LANDS row (a lure before the fight) and the lynx's waits for the fight, as LANDS 3.1 gives its snare (9a, the G35 row). Tracked to its bed for the bow stays the marten's (3.12).
- **Tell.** A silent kind, so the tell is a pose only: proposed, the crouch flattening, ears back, for two steps before it goes (BESTIARY 2, rule 9, asks a drawn tell of a silent kind); otherwise the cat is read by the still shape in cover; the lynx "holds still and watches, tufted ears, and chases only when the player is already close" (LANDS 3.1).
- **Fails.** Setting the snare at night, when it is awake; baiting with a carcass, which draws the canids and the crows instead; setting the box by day, within 2 tiles of the sleeping cat, which wakes it (HUNTING 2, SLEEP).
- **Sources.** BESTIARY 1 (wildcat: "baited snare or box trap"); LANDS 2 and 3.1; HUNTING 2 and 11; BEHAVIOUR 5; 3a (scent herb bait, tracking, box trap, passive traps).

### 3.7 Bears: the ice pit at the edge of the den ring

- **Style.** The white bear sleeps in its den ring by day (hours 20-07) and its gate is "avoid its ring of 8 tiles; late ice pit with bait" (BESTIARY 1). The pit (T5) is dug by day just outside the ring, so the digging noise (5) never reaches a bear with a dull ear (0) at 8 tiles, baited, and found at dawn. The river is a wall to the bear as to every hunter (water W, BEHAVIOUR 5), so the bank is no refuge from it on its round; the answer is to stay out of its ring (G34). The pit is taken before the fight, as LANDS 2 and BESTIARY 1 give it and HUNTING 8's "or the pit trap" allows (section 9). Proposed (3a): the bait is fish, the bears' bait type, and the pit is covered.
- **Alternative (proposed): the covered pit on its night path.** The bear's night route out of the ring carries its prints and signs for every player (3a, tracking: a route the server builds from its anchor, its roam circle and its own keyed draw, never the seed on a client, and sends to the players in view, read at dawn whether or not the bear was stepped; 9a, the seed row); in the highland's snow they would read fresher, which waits on climate (NATURE 2, a field with no table yet). A covered pit on that route, outside the ring, takes it with no bait (9a), resolved when the player checks it at dawn (3a, passive traps). It moves the hunt from the den, which a player learns once, to the bear's own round, which changes with the den's place.
- **Tell.** The den ring itself, seen by day; its alarm of 8 (BEHAVIOUR 5) when it wakes.
- **Fails.** Entering the ring; digging inside it, where the dig's noise of 5 reaches the den.
- **Sources.** BESTIARY 1; LANDS 2 (fight, lure: the ice pit); HUNTING 4 and 5; BEHAVIOUR 5; 3a (fish bait, pit cover, tracking, passive traps).

### 3.8 Game birds: the sling from beyond its sight

- **Style.** The sling (T2) reaches 6 tiles (HUNTING 5) and the ground fowl sees 5 (BESTIARY 1), so the fowl is the one bird slung from outside its own sight, with a sneak (3 + 1 = 4 tiles heard) that never reaches it. The dove sees 6 (LANDS 3.1), the sling's own reach, so it needs tall grass (2 off its sight) or its head down. The snare under the roost is the second way (LANDS 2, 3.1). Proposed (3a): the shaped stone, the sling's second grade, reaches 7, so the dove too is slung from outside its sight with no cover: the ammunition is the dove's rung on the ladder.
- **Alternative (proposed): the flush and the bola.** The dog (3a) points the flock and, sent in, puts it up away from itself, so the player stands on the far side. In the burst's window, its first 2 think steps (9a), a flushed bird may be struck by the bola (T3, range 5), the throw net (T2, range 3) or the hawk cast at it (3a, under question 12), and by no other strike (section 9a); traps take aware kinds by their own rule (9a). Wingshooting over a dog is the bird hunt the reference games build around a flush (theHunter's retriever and its upland birds). Before the dog, the early form is seed bait (3a) under a throw net, from a brush hide: the seed holds the fowl in GRAZE (sight 5 - 2 = 3), and the hide's 2 off makes it 1, inside the net's 3.
- **Tell.** The flock's burst: a clatter that is itself a noise of 5 others hear (LANDS 3.1, BEHAVIOUR 5, alarm 5). The dog's point: still, one paw up, nose to the flock (proposed, a held pose).
- **Fails.** One shot that misses lifts the whole flock of 5 to 12 (BEHAVIOUR 5, group); a bola thrown after the burst's window.
- **Sources.** HUNTING 3 and 5; BESTIARY 1; LANDS 2 and 3.1; BEHAVIOUR 5; 3a (shaped stone, dog, bola, throw net, hawk, seed bait, brush hide).

### 3.9 Perching birds: snared at a carcass

- **Style.** The crow comes to a carcass 30 steps after it is left (HUNTING 6; BEHAVIOUR 2, carcass), so the crow is taken with a snare at what the player left first; a sling stalk is possible (LANDS 2) but rarely pays: it is the valley's sentinel, with the sparrow and the fowl at alarm 5 (BEHAVIOUR 5), and lifts on the player's noise. The sparrow is not hunted; its moult under the perch is a resource node (LANDS 2).
- **Alternative (proposed): the owl decoy and the crow call.** Crows mob an owl: an owl decoy (T2, 3a) on a fence or a stump at the meadow's edge, with a reed crow call (T1), draws every crow within 12 tiles that has not sensed the player to circle within 3 tiles of the decoy for 6 steps. From a brush hide (2 off) 8 tiles from the decoy the crow's 7 is 5; the circle brings a crow to 6 tiles at times, inside the sling's 6 and outside its 5, and the shot is timed to that pass (the shaped stone's 7 widens it by a tile). The real practice is the crow hunter's decoy owl; the reference games' bird decoys are its game form (3a).
- **Tell.** The caw, an alarm of 5 that puts the hare and the deer up too (BEHAVIOUR 1, `alarm`; proposed, BEHAVIOUR 8, question 5, default A).
- **Fails.** A stalk in the open: the crow sees 7 and its burst is a noise of 5 others hear (HUNTING 8); a sling shot at the decoy's mob that misses, which ends the mob and puts the crows' alarm out across the meadow.
- **Sources.** HUNTING 6 and 8; LANDS 2 (crow: snare, stalk with a sling); BEHAVIOUR 1, 2, 5; 3a (owl decoy, crow call, brush hide, shaped stone).

### 3.10 Waterfowl and waders: from the reeds, past the watcher

- **Style.** The heron is taken with a thrown stone while it wades (LANDS 3.1) and it stands far more than it moves (weights 60/30/10, BEHAVIOUR 1). Its sight is 8 in front and 4 behind (`ceil(sight / 2)`, HUNTING 3); from the reeds' cover (2 off every sight) the rear radius is 2 if the 2 comes off after the halving (`ceil(8 / 2) - 2`), and the stone's 3 is thrown from outside it; if it comes off before (`ceil(6 / 2)`), the radius is 3, the stone's full reach, and the heron senses the thrower. HUNTING 3 does not give the order (section 9). A reed blind (proposed, 3a: 3 off every sight, at the water's edge) narrows it but does not settle it: behind the heron the radius is `ceil(8 / 2) - 3 = 1` one way and `ceil(5 / 2) = 3` the other, so the order stays the open point of section 9. The goose feeds in a skein where "one goose watches while the rest feed" (LANDS 3.1): the watcher is the one to beat, sight 7, 5 from tall grass, and the sling's 6 reaches past it.
- **Alternative (proposed): decoys and the honk call.** A skein on the move lands where its own kind already sits: two goose decoys (T2, 3a) on the water's edge and a reed honk call draw a flying skein within 12 tiles to land within 2 tiles of them. From a reed blind on the bank the watcher's 7 is 4, and the sling's 6 or the bow's 8 reaches past it. theHunter's coast reserve builds its waterfowl hunt on decoys and callers; this is the same hunt at a tile's size.
- **Tell.** The heron "lifts slowly, a tell of two steps"; the goose's honk is an alarm of 6 (LANDS 3.1, BEHAVIOUR 5). At the decoys: the skein's turn, a wide circle over the water before it lands (proposed, the flier's WANDER drawn as an arc).
- **Fails.** An approach in the open along the bank; a shot while the watcher faces the player; a decoy set where the skein's watcher sees the blind being built (3a: a blind is shunned for a game day).
- **Sources.** HUNTING 3 and 5; LANDS 3.1; BEHAVIOUR 1, 3 (the riverbank), 5; 3a (reed blind, goose decoy, honk call).

### 3.11 Raptors: the bait on the open crest

- **Style.** The eagle lives on the bare crest and is "never under the canopy" (LANDS 3.1); its ways are `fight` and a pit with bait (T5) after the fight. So the bait lies on the open crest and the player waits under the last trees, where it does not come. The spyglass (proposed, 3a) reads the eagle on its perch from 16 tiles, its sight of 10 being the longest of any hunted kind, so the bait is laid while the eagle is away.
- **Alternative (proposed, after the fight): the net by the owl decoy.** Raptors stoop on an owl as crows mob one: an owl decoy (3a) on the crest with a raised net beside it (T3, the bow net, 3a's purse net at a raptor's size) takes the eagle on its stoop, a trap as the pit is, so it waits for the fight as LANDS puts the eagle's pit. The real practice is the falconers' net by a decoy owl.
- **Tell.** "A stoop that stops a tile short" (LANDS 3.1), its bluff.
- **Fails.** Before the fight there is no take (G35); a bait laid while the eagle is on its perch.
- **Sources.** LANDS 3.1; HUNTING 5 and 11; 3a (spyglass, owl decoy, net).

### 3.12 Mustelids: the snare on the run off the ground

- **Style.** Each keeps one run: the otter its slide into the stream, the marten its branch (LANDS 3.1: otter "snare (at the slide)", marten "snare (on a branch)"). With the bat's net at its roost, these are the only snares set off the ground, and the only ones on a moving animal's run; the otter is also lured with a fish. Tracking (proposed, 3a) finds both runs: the slide is a sign on the bank that lasts a game day; the marten's run is its prints on the grove floor and a scratched trunk (in snow they would read fresher, once climate lands).
- **Alternative (proposed).** The otter: fish bait (3a) on the bank, a scent bait that draws it from 6 on the bait's downwind side (the otter's own smell is 2), and the sling (T2, 6) from a reed blind, where the otter's 5 is 2. The marten: tracked by day to the tree it sleeps in (hours 18-06), where in SLEEP it senses at `ceil(6 / 3) = 2`, and the bow's 8 or the sling's 6 takes it from the ground. Both are danger 0 and need no fight.
- **Tell.** The otter "slides into the water and is gone, a ring on the surface"; the marten's "scold from the branch above is its alarm" (LANDS 3.1).
- **Fails.** A ground snare; chasing an otter that has reached the water, where it is under for 8 steps (BEHAVIOUR 1, `flight` S).
- **Sources.** LANDS 3.1; HUNTING 2; BEHAVIOUR 1 and 5; 3a (tracking, fish bait, reed blind).

### 3.13 Fish: the line at the rise

- **Style.** The `fish` way, the rod (LANDS 1); the bone hook is the trout line's (MATERIALS 3.3, "bone hook (the trout line, BESTIARY)"), and BESTIARY 1 adds "spear in the shallows". Trout rise at dusk (the reaction `core/fish-at-dusk`, its cue `fish_rise`, BEHAVIOUR 3) and, proposed, at dawn (BEHAVIOUR 4).
- **Alternative (proposed): the trap and the weir.** A wicker fish trap (T1, 3a) on a water tile at the bank, baited, takes one fish a game day while its pool's stock lasts, checked as a snare is (Stardew Valley's crab pot is the reference: set, baited, checked daily). A stone weir (T3, 3a) across a stream 1 to 3 tiles wide turns the passing fish into its mouth: a trap in the mouth takes two a day, and the spear's throw of 4 (BESTIARY 1: "spear in the shallows") finds unaware fish there at any hour. Both stay inside the cap and the refill (12 / 1), and both count against the player's three passive traps (3a), so a player's traps empty a pool no faster than his line; the weir falls in 3 game days, its mouth trap pays only its builder, and the spear at its mouth is open to every player.
- **Tell.** The ripple ring (BESTIARY 1).
- **Fails.** Midday under the bank, when the fish are still; a trap with no bait; a weir across the whole river, which the check refuses (3a).
- **Sources.** BESTIARY 1; LANDS 1 and 2; MATERIALS 3.3; BEHAVIOUR 3 and 4; 3a (fish trap, weir).

### 3.14 Reptiles: the throw from the edge of its sight

- **Style.** The adder sees 2 (HUNTING 8), the shortest sight of any hunted kind, with the trout and the bat, and its way is "stalk (spear from 2 tiles), snare" (LANDS 2; BESTIARY 1: "snare on a rock"). The spear's 2 is the edge of its sight, so, as for the dove, the throw comes from cover (2 off) or while it basks with its head down, as the noticing count allows (HUNTING 3). It basks on a warm rock 06-20 and stays in when it rains (BEHAVIOUR 5, weather U). It is a defender, so until the fight it bluffs (HUNTING 11, question 1).
- **Alternative (proposed): the snare on its trail, set while it is in.** The adder lives on ruin, which holds no print (3a, tracking), so it is read by a sign of its own: its shed skin by the warm stone it basks on, a sign that lasts a game day. The snare is set on its trail tile between the rock it sleeps in and that stone, more than 2 tiles from the sleeping rock, on a rainy day or at night while it is in (weather U, BEHAVIOUR 5), and takes it when it comes out to bask, resolved when checked (3a, passive traps). A set within 2 tiles of the sleeping rock wakes it (HUNTING 2, SLEEP: "a player within 2 tiles -> ALERT"), and a woken defender bluffs; where the stone lies within 2 tiles of the rock there is no silent set, and the stalk is the only way. A snare is a trap, not a contact, so it needs no fight, as LANDS 2's snare already reads (9a, the G35 row).
- **Tell.** The coil on the warm stone (BESTIARY 1).
- **Fails.** A step within its 2 tiles; a rainy day, when it is not out (for the stalk; the rainy day is the snare's); a snare set within 2 tiles of its sleeping rock.
- **Sources.** HUNTING 2, 8 and 11; BESTIARY 1; LANDS 2; BEHAVIOUR 3 (ruin) and 5; 3a (tracking, passive traps).

### 3.15 Night fliers: the net at the roost by day

- **Style.** The bat hears every gait one tile further than any keen-eared kind (+3, LANDS 3.2; only the tomb spider matches it) and roosts under a roof (BEHAVIOUR 5), where it is night at every hour and the south's kinds run the night column by day (BEHAVIOUR 3 and 4). So the net goes up at the roost while the bat is out at the lamps, and is found when it comes back to its beam (LANDS 3.2: "snare (a net at the roost)"). Its second way, "lure (a lantern)", is read here as a lamp prop with the proposed `light` flag (BEHAVIOUR 2), placed by the map, not by the player: BEHAVIOUR 2 has bats drawn to a lamp within 8 at night and the carried lantern read by no animal (section 9). No player-placed lamp is designed.
- **Alternative (proposed): the net at the lamp.** The roost net moved to the light: by day, while the bat is at its beam, a purse net (3a) is hung on a map lamp's post, out of the hall it roosts in; at night the bat comes to a lamp within 8 (BEHAVIOUR 2) and circles it, and the net takes it at the light, found in the morning (resolved when checked, 3a, passive traps). No player stands at the lamp, so the ear of +3 is beaten by absence, not by stealth, as at the roost; it is the lamp lure LANDS 3.2 names, made into a take without a carried light. Fieldworkers net bats on their flight lines the same way.
- **Tell.** "Once it lifts the hall knows" (LANDS 3.2).
- **Fails.** Any approach at a walk under the roof while it is at its beam; a carried lantern, which no animal reads; a net hung at a lamp more than 8 tiles from the roost.
- **Sources.** LANDS 3.2; BEHAVIOUR 2 and 5; 3a (purse net, passive traps).

### 3.16 Horned beasts: after the fight, a coin by the brazier

- **Style.** Both members are `fight` first (LANDS 3.2); the imp's lure is "a coin in a snare, after the fight", the one bait that is neither food nor a light, laid by its brazier. The fiend has no `hunt` until tier 5 opens: a hazard escaped by construction.
- **Alternative (proposed, after the fight): the covered pit by the brazier.** The coin as bait over a covered pit (T5, 3a) on the imp's path between braziers, read by the spyglass from beyond its sight of 6. It is the pit the boar teaches, met again in the south.
- **Tell.** The imp's ember "that lands a tile short"; the fiend's horns at the descent (LANDS 3.2).
- **Fails.** Anything before the fight (G35).
- **Sources.** LANDS 3.2 and 6; 3a (pit cover, spyglass).

### 3.17 Spiders: never touch the web

- **Style.** The spider "feels the web, so a player who steps on a strand is noticed at any gait" (LANDS 3.2): its sense that matters is the strands, beyond its sight 3 and ear +3. The player reads the web and keeps off it; after the fight its lure is "a moth at the web", read here as a lamp prop near the web, with the proposed `light` flag and placed by the map, that draws the moths (BEHAVIOUR 2), state kinds with no `hunt` drawn as a cloud (BEHAVIOUR 5), since a moth is never taken (section 9).

It waits for the fight (danger 1).
- **Tell.** The strands across the hall's corner.
- **Fails.** One step on a strand.
- **Sources.** LANDS 3.2; BEHAVIOUR 2; 3a (smoke bundle).

### 3.18 Drakes: seen, not hunted

- **Style.** None in the first ring: a landmark seen from the stair, asleep on its ledge by day, with no `hunt` until tier 8 (LANDS 3.2; LANDS 6, question 2, default A). The spyglass (proposed, 3a) is how a player first sees it close.

## 3a. New tools, actions and gear (proposed)

Everything in this section is **proposed**. It is what the reference hunting games give a hunter and the valley lacks, cut to what reads at a phone's tile size (a prop, a pose, a ring, a print, an arrow on the screen's edge) and to what the kernel steps as an integer rule at the think step, with no float and no roll beyond the kernel's own keyed stream. Each tool's inputs come from the tiers before it, as HUNTING 5 asks ("each tool's inputs come from the tier before it, so the hunt climbs"); no tool is closed to a race (G70), none is sold for money (G39), and none answers a hazard (G34). The hard lines hold for every row: no blood on an animal, no suffering, the take is a fade and a drop of goods, no score of kills (HUNTING 10). Ranges are in tiles, times in think steps (8 ticks of 100 ms, HUNTING 1: 0.8 s) unless written as game days (`day_length_ms` 1200000, a 20-minute day of 1500 think steps).

| Tool or act | What it does in rules | Tier and inputs | Families | Reference | Rule it touches, and the proposed change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
