# A first set of making families - clean proposal

Built only from SURVEY.md (same folder; `[S#]` points to its source table) and the owner's rule:
no family is harder to develop than another, and going deep in a family makes life easier
*inside that family* - clothing for the clothier, weapons for the weapon maker, food for the cook.

## 1. The decision

Six families, split by **what the thing is for** (wear it, fight with it, eat it), not by material.
Metal is **not a family**: it arrives later as a material wave that adds metal rows to the families
that already make those things - armour to the clothing family, swords and shields to the weapons
family, tools to the tools family. Whoever advanced in clothing is already the armour maker when
iron comes. One shape, one experience curve and one set of tested limits for all. A player can
level every family but be a **specialist in only two at once**. Why by use: material splits strain
when a new material lands (New World's metal crunch [S18]); use-based splits (GW2) and mixed ones
(ESO's Clothing holds cloth *and* leather [S23]) take a new material without a new family.

## 2. The first set

| # | Family | Makes | Raw materials and where an explorer finds them | Stations and tools | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wear** | clothing, then armour: shirts, cloaks, boots, packs; later mail and plate | plant fibre (meadows, marsh), wool and hides (grazing lands, hunting), later metal | loom, tanning frame, sewing kit; later the forge for plates | day one |
| 2 | **Arms** | weapons and shields: club, spear, bow, arrows, wooden shield; later swords, axes, metal shields | wood (forests), bone and flint (hills, riverbeds), sinew (hunting), later metal | carving bench, fletching kit; later the forge | day one |
| 3 | **Kitchen** | food and drink: trail rations, warm meals, feasts | meat and fish (hunting, rivers, coast), fruit, roots, grain, salt (shores, flats) | campfire, then oven and smokehouse | day one |
| 4 | **Remedies** | potions, salves, antidotes, poisons, dyes | herbs, mushrooms, resins, minerals - many found only in one kind of land | mortar, still, drying rack | day one |
| 5 | **Fieldcraft** | tools and exploring gear: axe, pick, knife, sickle, rope, lantern, climbing hooks, raft, camp kit | wood, stone, fibre, fat and wax; later metal | workbench, ropewalk | day one |
| 6 | **Jewelcraft** | rings, amulets, gem settings, charms | precious metal (deep mines, riverbeds), gems (caves, cliffs), cord | jeweller's bench | with the metal wave |

**Gathering is not a family.** Anyone with the right tool gathers anything (GW2 [S28]); a better
Fieldcraft tool is faster, a worse one slower, never "cannot". Each family's small nodes raise the
yield of *its own* raw materials. **Preparing sits inside each family** (ESO [S23]): Wear spins and
tans, Kitchen salts and smokes. Smelting is one shared station with no XP of its own; the XP goes
to the family whose recipe uses the bar.

## 3. The shape every family shares ("12 + 2")

- **Levels 1-50 on one experience table**, identical for all families (ESO: one total for all seven
  lines [S23]; OSRS one table [S2]).
- **Five tiers**, opening at levels 1, 10, 20, 30, 40. Launch opens tiers 1-3 (cap 30); the metal
  wave opens tiers 4-5 for **all six families at once** (New World raised every cap together [S17]).
- **12 small nodes in 3 branches of 4.** The branches are the *same in every family*, only the flavour
  text differs. In each branch the nodes open at levels 5, 15, 25 and 35.
  - *Hands (speed):* -10% craft time; +1 batch size on preparing; -15% time; -15% time (total -40%).
  - *Thrift (materials):* +10% yield gathering the family's own raw materials; 10% chance to save one
    input; +10% yield; 10% save (totals +20% yield, 20% save).
  - *Finish (quality):* +5 points chance of a "fine" piece; +20% durability of made pieces; +5; +5.
- **Points:** one family point at each of levels 5, 10, ..., 45 = **9 points for 12 nodes**. Everyone
  who levels a family picks 9 of 12, so two players at level 50 can still differ.
- **2 big nodes, in one of two paths.** Each family has two *paths* named by use, not material (see
  the table at the end). A path has a big node at level 25 and its master node at level 45 (live from the metal wave). A player
  takes one path per family. Big nodes need a **deep slot** (below).
- **What a big node may give:** (a) the *masterwork* grade, at most +10% to the piece's main number
  over a "fine" piece; (b) two signature recipes per tier from tier 3 up - side-grades in looks or
  trade-offs, never strictly stronger; (c) one rule change, for example "taking apart your own
  pieces returns half the materials" or "your pieces repair free for you".
- **Forbidden on any node:** stats on the player (damage, health) - WoW pulled those in 6.0 [S12];
  any effect on another family; XP boosts; exclusive regions, nodes or materials; any penalty for
  not being a specialist; a recipe that is the only road to a tier's main piece; stacking past the caps.

**Borrowed from:** the shared curve from ESO, FFXIV and OSRS; many small nodes and few big ones from
WoW's specialisation trees [S9] and Path of Exile 2 [S31], cut down to 14; the two-way path from
Stardew's professions [S30]; the quality edge from Albion [S13]. Readable on a phone screen, yet
two level-50 players still differ.

## 4. Parity rule - checks a family must pass to ship

| # | Check | Pass when |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Experience table and tier levels | identical to the shared table, no exceptions |
| P2 | First useful piece | 5-15 min for a fresh character in the starting region, gathering included, starter tools only, at most 3 raw material kinds |
| P3 | Time per tier | median test-player hours from one tier to the next within ±15% of the all-family median; same for 1→30 |
| P4 | Gathering per 1,000 XP | within ±15% of the median, measured in **minutes**, not item counts |
| P5 | Preparation steps for a tier's main piece | 3 ± 1 (raw → prepared → part → piece) |
| P6 | Pieces per tier | 8 ± 2; colour or style variants of one base piece count once |
| P7 | Node budget | exactly 12 small + 2 paths × 2 big; small node numbers copied from the shared branches |
| P8 | Supply | each tier's raw materials appear in at least 2 regions and 2 node kinds; no single node kind supplies more than half a tier's XP (New World's metal lesson [S18]) |
| P9 | Borrowed parts | at most 1 per piece, none in tier 1, lender's part from its first 10 levels of that tier or sold by a vendor |
| P10 | Specialist edge | masterwork at most +10% over fine; a non-specialist's best piece of a tier reaches at least 90% of a specialist's |

P3 and P4 are where survey games failed on one XP table (OSRS about 7 to 1 in hours, WoW 1.55 to 1
in weekly knowledge [S10]). Measure them in play tests and again two weeks after launch.

## 5. Specialist and non-specialist

- **Everyone** can make every standard recipe up to their level, the top piece of each tier included,
  at "normal" or "fine" grade, and gets the 9 small nodes by levelling: up to 40% faster, 20% fewer
  materials, 20% more gathering. That is the owner's "easier inside the family", with no slot needed.
- **The specialist** (family in a deep slot) adds the path's two big nodes: masterwork grade,
  signatures, one rule change. Only the top piece's best *grade* is specialist-only; a small edge
  keeps everyone crafting (FFXIV [S21]).

## 6. How many families deep - two

**2 deep slots** per player; levels and small nodes are unlimited. Moving a slot keeps all progress
(the old family's big nodes sleep) and is allowed once every 7 days (GW2 keeps progress on a swap
[S27]). Why two: of six families it leaves four to trade for - what made WoW's two primaries [S7]
and GW2's two active disciplines markets. Three would make most players self-sufficient; one would
feel like a class lock in an exploring game.

## 7. How families lean on each other - at most one borrowed part per piece

| Family | Borrows (from tier 2 up) | From |
|---|---|---|
| Wear | a fastening: toggle, later a buckle | Fieldcraft |
| Arms | a grip wrap or bowstring | Wear |
| Kitchen | preserving salt or spice, for trail food only | Remedies |
| Remedies | a vial or flask | Fieldcraft |
| Fieldcraft | a strap or harness | Wear |
| Jewelcraft | a cord or polish | Wear or Remedies |

Each part is cheap, early in the lender's tier and sold by a vendor (P9), so nobody must master
another family - FFXIV's web of parts is the warning [S22]. Food may add at most 5% craft speed;
Kitchen's real job is exploring food: warmth, stamina, carrying.

## 8. How exploring feeds the families

- **Materials by region.** Each tier lives one band further out (Valheim's biome ladder [S29]), each
  material in at least two regions (P8). Rare regional kinds - a frost hide, a salt-marsh herb - feed
  signature recipes, so every region is worth a visit for every family.
- **Great stations in far places.** Each family has exactly 3 - an old forge in a volcano, a giant
  loom in a ruin, a hot-spring dye works - one in tier-3 lands, two in tier-4/5 lands. Masterwork at
  tiers 4-5 needs one (ESO's set stations in the wild [S26]). Daily crafting stays at camps and towns.
- **Recipes found by exploring.** About half come with levels, half are found: ruin carvings, people
  on the road, studying a found item (ESO research [S25], in hours, not weeks). Found recipes are
  side-grades and regional styles, never a tier's main piece, so progress never hangs on luck.
- **A camp kit** lets anyone make tier 1-2 recipes in the field.

## 9. How metal plugs in without making leather and cloth worthless

1. **A wave, not a family.** Metal adds rows to Wear (mail, plate), Arms (blades, metal shields,
   arrowheads), Fieldcraft (tools); Kitchen and Remedies get metal pots and stills. Launch tiers ship
   with 6-7 pieces so every family stays inside 8 ± 2 after the wave, and tiers 4-5 open for all.
2. **It reaches down to level 1.** Copper and bronze rows land in tiers 1-3 in existing regions, so a
   late starter meets metal early (OSRS Sailing came in as a full 1-99 skill using older skills [S6]).
3. **Three armour weights, each best at something.** Cloth: stamina, stealth, warmth. Leather:
   balance, quiet movement. Metal: protection, paid in weight and noise (GW2 mirrors its three
   weights [S27]).
4. **New cloth and leather in every tier** - GW2 gives every tier a cloth, leather, metal and wood [S27].
5. **Metal pieces borrow leather or cloth** (padding, straps) as their one part - steady demand.
6. **Nothing is taken away.** Jewelcraft starts at level 1 on the same curve; no recipe leaves an
   older family (Albion converted progress to "equal to or better" [S13]).

## 10. The three biggest risks and the early sign of each

| Risk | Early sign |
|---|---|
| **A hidden time gap** - equal on paper, one family much slower in play (OSRS, New World) | in the first two weeks, any family's median hours to level 20 more than 25% off the median, or one family's raw material selling at over twice the others' |
| **Metal swallows cloth and leather** after the wave | over 60% of players wearing metal in every slot, or top-tier cloth and leather crafts falling by half week on week |
| **The specialist edge is the wrong size** - too big forces buying, too small leaves no reason to specialise | under 20% of top-tier pieces in use are non-masterwork (too big); masterwork selling under 10% above fine, or under 30% of eligible players filling a deep slot (too small) |

## 11. Two variants

- **Smaller - 4:** Wear; Arms (takes Jewelcraft at the wave); Provisions (Kitchen + Remedies);
  Fieldcraft. Same shape, 1 deep slot. Better for a small team, a short content runway, or a solo
  or small co-op game where few players means few markets.
- **Wider - 11:** Tailoring, Leatherwork, Armoursmith, Bowyer, Weaponsmith, Kitchen, Brewing, Remedies,
  Fieldcraft, Jewelcraft, Building; still 2 deep slots (WoW: 2 of 11 [S7]). Better for a large shared
  world where trade is a pillar - while every family keeps above about 5% of active players.
