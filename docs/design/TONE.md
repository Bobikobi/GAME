# Tone and register: the line at PEGI 16

**Status:** The line answered 19.10 (DECISIONS 2026-10-23 "The line is PEGI 16 and ESRB T", carried from #223); the rule lines it changes (G22, G33, G46 and checklist question 11) are rewritten in the next pull request.

The owner, 19.10: "as mature as it can be, not necessarily sexual, but brutal
in taste." Three lines were put to him with the games and the ratings on each,
and he chose the second:

| Line | Games on it | Rating | What it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| A hard world told softly | Minecraft, Stardew Valley, RuneScape | PEGI 7 to 12, ESRB E10+ to T | Danger by shape and sound; "you fell"; no blood on a person (the line of 10.10). |
| **Brutal in taste, never for show** | Dark Souls, Darkest Dungeon, Final Fantasy XIV | **PEGI 16, ESRB T** | Death is named, blood and bodies are in the scene, hard speech by character; nothing close up, nothing dwelt on, nothing sexual. |
| Brutal on the screen | The Witcher 3, Red Dead Redemption 2, Blasphemous | PEGI 18, ESRB M | Gore, torture, sex, the strongest language. Not chosen. |

Two ratings, and the tighter one holds. PEGI 16 is where violence "looks the
same as would be expected in real life", where bad language "can be more
extreme" and where alcohol may appear; PEGI 18 is gross violence, violence
against someone who cannot act, sexual violence and, since 2020, simulated
gambling. ESRB T (13 and up) allows violence, minimal blood, crude humour,
infrequent strong language and simulated gambling; ESRB M (17 and up) is
intense violence, blood and gore, sexual content and strong language. Dark
Souls and Elden Ring carry PEGI 16 in Europe and M in America, for gore; Final
Fantasy XIV and Darkest Dungeon carry 16 and T. The aim is 16 and T: the Dark
Souls taste, with blood kept small enough for T. The account stays at 13 or
the country's higher age (DECISIONS.md, 9.10): T is 13, and PEGI is advice to
parents, binding only for boxed sale in a few countries.

This page is the measure. G22 holds the words, G46 the images, G33 the death;
the three scenes of section 4 show the line at the sprite's size and in two
lines a box (G14). [STORY.md](STORY.md) S1 to S6 do not change: the beliefs,
the folds and the returners are told the same way, only harder.

## 1. What changes, what stays

| | Until 19.10 | From 19.10 |

## 2. The register: the words

Plain stays plain. Short sentences, concrete nouns, the simplest word that is
exact, and a reader whose English is their second language follows every line.
What G22 drops is the reader of eight as the measure; what it keeps is
everything that made a line readable on a phone in two lines.

- **Death is named death.** The card says "You died." It is the one line in
  the past tense, because it is the only thing that is over. The narrator,
  the journal and the characters say died, killed, dead and bled when that is
  what happened; never softer, never bigger.
- **Blood and wounds are named when they are in the scene** and never named
  to decorate: "Teeth in your arm" is a fact the player needs; "blood sprays
  across the grass" is not.
- **Hard speech is by sheet.** Each voice sheet (GAME_RULES.md, the voices
  table) says whether a character curses and at what: Pell at prices and
  roads, never at people; Maru never; the narrator never; signs never. A new
  character's sheet says it before a line is written.
- **The words on the stall**, at most one in a scene and never two in a box:
  damn, hell, bastard, bloody, piss, shit. Not on it: sexual swearing, the
  f-word, any slur (origin, sex, religion, body, mind), and an oath on a real
  religion; the valley swears by its own things, the bells and the mist.
- **Nothing sexual, nothing leering.** No innuendo from the narrator, no line
  about a body, no romance that is more than two sentences and a closed door.
- **Cruelty is spoken of, never relished.** A character may tell what the
  mist did to the miller; no line enjoys it, and no line asks the player to.
- **Alcohol is a thing of the inn** (the owner, 19.10: beer and the night's
  food): sold, drunk, with its cost on the morning, never a reward and never
  a joke about a drunk child. A drunk character at the inn is a night face,
  not a lesson.
- **The gamble quest kind wagers effort**, a day's work against a chance,
  never money at a table and never a game of cards: PEGI 18 is where
  simulated gambling went in 2020.

## 3. The images

- **What may show**, at the sprite's own size: blood where a hit lands, on a
  person too; a wound that stays as a bandage, a torn sleeve or a scar; a
  predator drawn with its teeth in the player; a body on the ground, the
  player's for the moment the card is up, an animal's or a stranger's as part
  of a place; bones by a ruin; a trail of blood where a hurt animal went.
- **The limit, which is the rating's own:** nothing close up, nothing the
  camera, a sound or the screen dwells on; no gore for its own sake, no
  dismemberment, no torture played out; no violence against someone who
  cannot act (a child, a bound person, a sleeper); nothing sexual at all.
- **Small and brief.** Blood is a few pixels and fades with the scene; a body
  lies for the card's seconds (the player's) or stays as a prop (a stranger's,
  bones, an animal's), never animated in pain.
- **The art is a direction decision.** A predator drawn biting, a body in the
  grass, blood on a sleeve: these change every sprite sheet that has them, so
  two or three phone-size stills of one scene go to the owner before a sprite
  is touched, as for every look. Until then the sprites stay as built.

## 4. Three scenes at the line

Each is a sample for the measure, not content: the lines enter the game
through the content files and their checks (STORY S-1, the forge) when their
beat is built. Two lines a box, one box a beat (G14); the narrator in the
second person, present tense (the voices table).

**The bank wolf (the stealth beat).** Until now: the hit shows the health it
takes and the box says "Run." From now:

> *(the wolf's jaws close on the arm; a red mark on the sleeve, at the sprite's size)*
> Teeth in your arm. The wolf has you.
> Run. It does not let go on its own.
> *(in town, the sleeve torn; Pell, counting)* Bite marks. The bank wolf. Lucky it hunts alone.

**The death (the combat and fall frame, the order's third item).** Until now:
"You fell", crosses for eyes, the body sinks, nothing left. From now:

> *(the body falls where it stood and lies in the grass; the screen darkens over two seconds)*
> **You died.**
> *(the square, at whatever hour it is; the narrator)* You wake in the square. Your pack lies where you died.
> *(Maru, slow)* You came back. Not all do.

The pack is G33's: the outing's finds, where the body lay, anyone's to take,
gone a game day later.

**Pell's hard telling (lines with conditions, S-1).** Until now, the Wakers'
telling: "Some say the bell is a waking call." From now, one of her tellings
for a player who has leaned nowhere yet:

> A carter told me. They found the miller past the arch, two nights in the mist.
> What walked back was not him. That one's free. The next costs.

Pell keeps her sheet: it is a traveller's story, counted and priced, with no
lore of her own and no joke about the miller.

## 5. What the check holds

- A line with a word off the stall (section 2) is an error in `pnpm check`;
  the list is the one the players' filter uses for slurs and sexual words,
  kept in one place.
- Two strong words in one box, or more than one in a scene, is a warning for
  a person to read.
- A narrator's line that says what to feel, or enjoys a hurt ("deliciously",
  "a satisfying crunch"), is a warning (the voices table's "never").
- The review checklist's question 11 (GAME_RULES.md section 7) reads the
  images against section 3.
- A store's rating that comes back at 18 or M is answered by cutting the
  scene that pushed it, never by moving the line.
