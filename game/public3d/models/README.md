## Animals (`animal-*`)

Two bodies for the first animals of the hunting foundations, both CC0 (public domain) from
[OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org), so the opening line of this README does not cover them
either (they are not Kenney's). Each carries its own clips and is posed with them and drawn to
pixels by `tools/anim` (targets `hare` and `crow` in `tools/anim/anim.json`); CC0 asks for no
attribution, the credit is here because the valley names everything it uses.

- `animal-hare.glb`: "Rabbit" by CDmir (with TinyWorlds), https://opengameart.org/content/rabbit-0, licensed [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); a rigged rabbit (an IK rig) with Guarding, Running, Jump, Sitting and Dying actions; attribution: "Rabbit by CDmir (with TinyWorlds), OpenGameArt, CC0, https://opengameart.org/content/rabbit-0; modified: the fur-shell mesh, the lights, the camera and the ground plane removed; the Blender Internal material rebuilt as a Principled BSDF on the packed colour map, warmed and lightened toward a sandy hare; scaled to 0.35 m at the ears with the scale baked into the bones, and a tip bone under each paw (the renderer reads a foot from its bone's tail); Guarding renamed Idle; Walk and Run made from Running in Blender (game/tools/blender/hare_pass.py: Walk 1.6x slower with the swings and the bob at 0.3 of the bound's around their means and the paws' lift at 0.55 of it, Run at the bound's tempo with them at 0.5 and the lift at 0.7, the planted paws kept on the ground); Alert, Graze and Lie keyed in Blender on the rig's own controls (the same script: Guarding's sit-up with the ears pricked and the head turning, its crouch with the nose on the floor, and Dying's last frame only, rolled onto its side with the legs drawn in); the other actions dropped; posed with its own actions and rendered to pixels by tools/anim".

## Props (`prop-*`)

`prop-spade.glb`, `prop-basket.glb` and `prop-hat.glb` are not from Kenney either: they were made
for this repository in Blender by `game/tools/blender/props.py`, which draws their shapes and every
colour and normal map in code, so they hold no outside asset and need no attribution. Each names in
its node extras the socket it hangs from on a person rigged by `game/tools/blender/person_rig.py`
(`socket_back` for the spade and the basket, `socket_head` for the hat), and each was fitted to the
people who carry it: the spade to `character-digger-m.glb` and `character-digger-f.glb`, the
basket and its straps to `character-forager-m.glb` and `character-forager-f.glb`, and the hat to
`character-explorer-m.glb` and `character-explorer-f.glb`. What lies on the body, the straps and the
hat, is fitted to each person in turn, and the file holds one copy per person, named by the look in
its node extras (`wearer`). The cell trial puts the props on by role (`?look=digger-f`,
`?look=forager-m`, `?look=explorer-f`) or by `?props=`, with the copy of that look (the first copy on
any other).
